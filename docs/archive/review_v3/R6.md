## 1. For the owner, in plain language

**What makes drawing slow is mostly not what we expected.**

- **The graphics card's side of the browser is the bottleneck, and shadows are
  most of its work.** Every Token, hero, flag and enemy has a small drop shadow
  under it, drawn by a CSS "filter". At the realistic board (S2, ~100 Tokens) the
  browser's graphics process was busy **~95 % of the time**, and the board drew
  only **~100–125 frames a second** instead of 165. Switching the mat's filters
  off (as an experiment only) let it reach **164 frames a second**, graphics
  busy **53 %**, and the share of frames that fit in 6.06 ms went from **59 % to
  89 %**. That is the single biggest lever. **But it changes how things look**,
  so it is your call (question R6-Q1). There are ways to keep the shadows and
  lose most of the cost (draw the shadow as a picture instead of a filter).
- **Walking heroes moved with `left`/`top` costs less than the plan feared** on
  the page itself (a quarter of a millisecond a frame), but it does make the
  graphics side redraw the ground under every walker. Moving walkers with
  `transform` instead lifted S2 from ~100 to ~125 frames a second. **Only the
  things that walk should switch**: switching every Token made the 300-Token
  board slightly *worse*.
- **Four invisible fixes add up.** Letting the particle canvas sleep when no loot
  is flying (the biggest single one on the page itself), one shared clock for
  the progress rings that also skips redraws nobody could see, and drawing hero
  animation frames without React. Together with transform-walking, S2 went
  from **38 % to 73 %** of frames within 6.06 ms and from ~99 to ~151 frames a
  second, and the "MatBoard redraws a second" counter fell from **40 to 8**.
  None of these is meant to change what you see.
- **One thing looked like a cheap fix and is a trap:** telling the browser to give
  each sprite its own layer (`will-change`) made S2 **3× slower** (43 frames a
  second). It is written into the draft rules so nobody tries it.
- **At 300 Tokens (S3) the page itself runs out of room** — about 10–13 ms of
  main-thread work per frame, half of it the browser laying out and painting
  2,500 page elements, the rest the game's rules and React. The cheap fixes help
  (fps 65 → 80) but do not come close to the S3 target. Whether that means
  "draw the mat on a canvas" is a big decision and **not one to make yet**
  (question R6-Q3): the engine's own S3 cliff (P2) has to be fixed first, and
  Session C has to measure a production build.
- **A real bug found on the way:** past ~225 Tokens-plus-flags the mat's layer
  numbers run out, so at S3 **95 Tokens share one layer** — including every
  Token a hero is working, which should draw in front. (CR3-354.)

**What you should look at by eye, if the fix waves go ahead** (each spike was
built to look identical; the eye is the judge):

1. **Walking heroes, walking goblins and speech bubbles** (transform walking,
   CR3-007): do the pixel sprites stay crisp *while moving*, or look slightly
   soft? Do bubbles still sit over the head and follow the walk?
2. **The progress ring** (CR3-351): does it still sweep smoothly?
3. **Hero animations** (CR3-301, R5's ticket, measured here): do heroes still
   swing on the same beat as the Token's hit reaction, and does a limping hero
   still walk slowly?
4. **Loot flying to the Hall** (CR3-010): the first sparkle after a quiet spell
   still appears at once.
5. If you choose a shadow option in R6-Q1: the shadows themselves.

## 3. Frame-time anatomy (S2 and S3, before any change)

Measured on `main` code; per frame, averaged over two interleaved runs.
"Frame work" is P3's estimate (rAF to the first task after the frame) — main
thread only. The trace columns are self-time per rendering stage on the page's
main thread, plus the other threads that draw.

| | S1 Quiet (7 Tokens) | S2 Realistic (102 Tokens, 8 heroes) | S3 Torture (310–314 Tokens) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Elements in the mat / composited layers | ~260 page | 1,009 / 85 | 2,528 / 233 |
| Frame work p50 / p99 | 1.2 / 2.1 ms | 5.6 / 12.9 ms (round 2: 6.7 / 14.9) | 9.2 / 68 ms (round 2: 11.2 / 105) |
| Frames ≤ 6.06 ms | 100 % | 59 % (38 %) | 2 % (0 %) |
| Frames drawn per second | 165 (vsync) | **~100–125** | 65–90 |
| Main thread per frame | 1.7 ms | **5.0 ms** | **9.8–12.9 ms** |
| … script (React, engine tick, rAF loops) | 0.35 | 1.31 | 2.7–4.3 |
| … style (incl. CSS animations) | 0.42 | 1.19 | 1.6–2.0 |
| … layout | 0.01 | **0.23** | 1.0–1.2 |
| … paint (recording) | 0.41 | 1.48 | 3.3–3.9 |
| … compositing update | 0.16 | 0.28 (+ PaintArtifactCompositor, ~0.6, in "other") | 0.6 (+ ~2) |
| Page's compositor thread | — | 2.0 ms | 3.4 ms |
| **Graphics process main thread busy** | 29 % | **~95 %** | ~90 % |
| Long animation frames / 30 s | 0 | 1–2 (React scheduler) | 20–35 (**engine tick** `GameLoop.js` setInterval ≈ 2/3, React ≈ 1/3) |
| MatBoard Profiler commits / s | — | 40–43 | 34–38 |

What this says:

1. **At S2 the frame rate is capped by the graphics process, not the page.** It
   sits at 90–100 % busy while the page's main thread is ~50 % busy. Its time
   goes to compositing render passes (`FinishPaintRenderPass`, ~45 %) and
   rasterising (`DoEndRaster…Flush` + deserialising, ~50 %). Filters make both:
   each `drop-shadow` is a separate render pass or a re-raster of every tile a
   moving thing crosses.
2. **Layout is small at S2** (0.23 ms/frame) — CR3-007's layout claim is right in
   kind and small in size; its GPU side matters more (§4).
3. **Something changes every frame even on a calm board**, which is why style
   and paint never drop to zero: 6–7 cycle rings repaint their SVG arc every
   frame, 2 tutorial beacons poll their target's rect every frame and animate a
   border width (a layout property), the particle canvas clears 1600×900 every
   frame, 8 heroes flip sprite frames through React 8×/s (plus 8 more in the
   dock), 14 elements breathe a filter glow, 7 worked Tokens loop a hit
   animation, and charge floaters animate on framer-motion's JS loop.
4. **S3 is a different problem.** Its long frames are mostly the **engine tick**
   (P2's S3 cliff: CR3-001/003/004/047 — engine sessions), and the page's own
   paint + compositing work roughly doubles again with element count (§5).

**Timers (CR3-011), counted at runtime, S2:** 7 live intervals in the whole page
— `GameLoop` 100 ms, `HeroBubbleLayer` 500 ms, `MatUpkeepBadge` 2 s, autosave
30 s, notification heartbeat 10 s, and two from the Perf HUD itself. **No
`TurnRing` (none turning at S2), no enemy sprite interval.** Per-frame loops:
**6–7 `TokenBadgeRow` ring loops** (one per worked Token), **2 `TutorialBeacon`
loops**, ParticleOverlay, framer-motion's frame loop, FPSCounter and the Perf HUD
(dev only) — about **12 rAF callbacks a frame**. Plus 8 hero `setTimeout` chains
at 8/s. S3 is the same set (7–8 ring loops).

## 5. The element ceiling (plan §2.F), and the canvas question

Three points, same machine, dev build:

| | Tokens | Elements in mat | Layers | Main thread / frame | of which render stages (style + layout + paint + compositing + other) | GPU busy |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | 7 | ~200 | — | 1.7 ms | 1.35 ms | 29 % |
| S2 | 102 | 1,009 | 85 | 5.0 ms (3.9 after cheap set) | 3.7 ms | 95 % (81 %) |
| S3 | 310 | 2,528 | 233 | 9.8–12.9 ms (10.2 after) | 6.5–8.5 ms | 90 % (84 %) |

- The page's render stages grow **roughly linearly: ~0.02 ms per Token per
  frame**, on top of ~1.3 ms fixed. On their own they would fill 6.06 ms at
  roughly **200–230 Tokens**, before any script runs. Composited layers grow
  ~0.7 per Token (overlap with animated things forces neighbours onto layers).
- **Where compositing dominates:** on the graphics side, *already at S2*, because
  of the filters; on the page side, past ~200 Tokens.
- **So:** with the cheap fixes and a shadow decision, **S2 plausibly reaches its
  target** in a production build (the page's own script roughly halves without
  React's development mode; 4c alone reached 89 % in dev). **S3 does not
  plausibly reach 95 %** with the element-based mat on this evidence: even with
  every filter off it was 48 %, its render stages alone are ~7 ms, and its long
  frames are dominated by the engine tick (P2's S3 cliff), which the engine
  sessions own.

That meets the plan's condition for putting the canvas question to the owner —
but the honest recommendation is **not to decide it yet** (R6-Q3), because two
large unknowns sit in front of it: the engine's S3 fixes and a production-build
measurement. Filed as **CR3-355** with the costs.

## 6. Tickets

### CR3-350 — Drop-shadow filters are the graphics card's biggest cost; the frame rate is capped by them
- **Category / Severity / Effort**: PNT · **P1** · M
- **Impact / Confidence / Score**: 5 · 1.0 (measured) · 2.5

## 8. Draft: the mat-era performance rules (CR3-021)

*A replacement for `.agent/guides/PERFORMANCE.md`, drafted here, not in place.
R7's drag section (`docs/review_v3/R7.md` §4) slots in as its own chapter.
Everything below is measured in this session or carried over where it still
holds.*

### The budget

- **Drawing runs at the screen's rate (165 Hz = 6.06 ms a frame); the rules run
  at 10 ticks a second** (owner ruling Q1). The 6.06 ms is shared by everything
  the browser does for a frame: script, style, layout, paint, compositing — and
  the **graphics process**, which has its own budget and on the mat is the first
  to run out.
- Measure with `?stress=realistic` / `?stress=torture` and `window.__perf`
  (`docs/review_v3/P3.md`). On a 60 Hz screen read `frameWork`, not `frames`.
  The HUD's "MatBoard" line counts the whole mat subtree (CR3-356).

### Rules

1. **Nothing runs every frame unless something on screen is moving.** A
   `requestAnimationFrame` loop, an interval or an `infinite` CSS animation must
   stop when it has nothing to show. Idle mat = idle browser. (Particle canvas,
   beacons, ring clock.)
2. **One frame clock.** Per-frame work (progress rings, anything interpolated
   between ticks) subscribes to one shared loop (`frameClock.onFrame`) that stops
   with no subscribers. No component starts its own loop.
3. **Write to the page only when pixels change.** An imperative painter compares
   with what it last wrote and skips a write that moves nothing (the ring moves
   in 400 steps, not 165 a second).
4. **Clocks do not go through React.** A sprite-sheet frame, a countdown, a
   progress sweep is written to its element (a ref, the Web Animations API, or a
   CSS `steps()` animation). React state is for what the player's choices
   change. Hero frames through React were 64 commits a second on the mat and
   64 in the dock.
5. **Things that walk move by `transform`; things that stand use `left`/`top`.**
   A walker's box is placed with `transform: translate(x, y)` and glides with a
   `transform` transition. Do **not** put every Token on `transform`: at 300
   Tokens that costs more in compositing than it saves in layout. Decide by the
   Token's kind, never mid-life. Keep whole pixels (D-216).
6. **Filters are the graphics card's work.** Each `drop-shadow` is a render pass
   or a re-raster whenever something near it changes. Prefer shadows and glows
   drawn as images; animate a glow's `opacity`, never the filter's values. **Never
   add `will-change` to sprites** to "help": each filtered sprite then becomes
   its own render pass (measured 3× slower). Never add `transition: filter`
   "just in case".
7. **Events carry what changed; selectors read by id.** Subscribe to the
   narrowest event; `state_changed` is the last resort; per-Token subscriptions
   go through `tokenEvents.js` (one bus listener for N Tokens). (R5 owns the
   details.)
8. **Only what changed re-renders.** `React.memo` on every mat component fed by
   `MatBoard`; stable handler props (`useCallback`); stable arrays and maps
   passed as props. A walk step or a sprite frame must not re-render the mat.
9. **No layout reads in hot paths.** No `getBoundingClientRect`, `offsetWidth`
   or query-then-measure per frame, per pointer move, or in a `useLayoutEffect`
   without dependencies. Cache the mat's rect on pointer-enter and resize.
10. **Layers are a budget too.** The mat's z-order has a fixed range
    (`matLayers.js`); anything that adds ranks must hold at S3 size.
11. **Dev loops stay dev-only and switch off while measuring** (FPS counter, HUD).
12. **Every perf change carries a before/after number** from the same scenario,
    window size and build, measured back to back, and says what the owner should
    look at by eye.
13. **Anything drawn once per Token is measured at S3** (~310 Tokens) before it
    merges. Its cost must grow no faster than the Token count.

### Still true from the card-era guide

- Specific events over `state_changed` (the event names changed: see R5's table,
  `R5.md:87` — `cards_updated` and `quest_state_changed` have no publisher now).
- Mutations go through managers, which publish the right events.
- No `console.log` in hot paths.
