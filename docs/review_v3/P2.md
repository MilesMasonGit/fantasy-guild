# P2 — The headless engine benchmark (Tier A)

*Build session, 2026-09-28, branch `tool/bench`. No game code changed.*
*⚠ **All numbers here are provisional.** They were taken while two review agents
were reading the same checkout and using the CPU. The director should re-take
the baseline when the machine is quiet (`npm run bench -- --save-baseline`).*

Machine: Intel i7-8700 @ 3.20 GHz, 12 threads, Windows 10, Node v24.11.1
(`DESKTOP-IEM8MOS`). Commit measured: `5adc056` plus the bench itself.

---

## In plain language

**What was built.** A command, `npm run bench`, that runs the game's engine — the
part that decides what heroes do, what Tokens make, where enemies walk — without
the screen. It builds six test boards, runs thousands of game ticks as fast as
it can, and reports how long each tick took. It takes about three minutes.

The game ticks ten times a second. Each tick has to fit inside one screen frame,
and at 165 frames a second a frame is only 6 ms — so a tick that takes 4 ms
eats two-thirds of the frame it lands in, ten times a second. The plan's targets
are: on a realistic late-game board, **99 ticks in 100 under 1.5 ms**, and none
over 4 ms.

It also has a **tripwire**: `npm run bench -- --compare` compares a run with a
saved reference run and fails if anything got more than 20 % slower. This was
proven by planting a deliberate 0.5 ms slowdown: the tripwire caught it; without
the slowdown it stayed quiet.

**What the first numbers say.**

1. **A realistic late-game board already misses the target, by about 2×.** The
   typical tick is 1.3 ms (fine), but the one-in-a-hundred slow tick is
   **3.4 ms** (target 1.5) and the worst was **17 ms** (target 4). A player
   would see that as a small stutter every ten seconds or so.
2. **More than half of every tick is the same list being rebuilt again and
   again.** The engine asks "give me every Token on the mat, in order" **47 times
   per tick** on the realistic board, and 131 times on the big one. Each time it
   copies, filters and sorts the whole list. That alone is ~60 % of the tick
   (CR3-001, confirmed).
3. **Spawners that are already full keep trying to spawn every tick** — a
   surprise, not on the pre-filed list. That is the biggest single stage on the
   realistic board (0.8 ms of 1.4).
4. **One "whole mat" buff turns small stutters into big ones.** Add a single
   board-reach aura to the realistic board and the one-in-a-hundred tick goes
   from 3.4 ms to **14.5 ms**. On a 300-Token board with one such aura, ticks of
   **70 ms** happen every ten seconds and the worst was **246 ms** — a quarter of
   a second frozen (CR3-004, confirmed).
5. **Dropping Tokens into a crowded spot is slow.** When a spawner has to push
   its neighbours aside, one arrival takes **28 ms typically, 59 ms worst**
   (target 8 ms). Shrinking the mat from 20 to 6 steps took **347 ms**
   (CR3-003, confirmed).
6. **No memory leak seen in 8 game-hours.** Memory after clean-up was flat
   (+0.3 % between hour 1 and hour 8), and every structure the bench can see
   stayed the same size.
7. **Two suspects were cleared.** Heroes re-searching for work when the Bank
   changes (CR3-002) and item-threshold triggers (CR3-029) cost almost nothing
   (under 1 % of a tick).

None of this touches drawing. How long the *screen* takes is Tier B (P3).

## Surprises worth a ticket (for the R-sessions, not filed here)

1. **Full spawners retry every tick.** A spawner at its family cap holds its
   clock full and `attemptSpawn` runs again next tick, forever (7.2 calls per
   tick on S2 with 8 spawners). Each attempt counts the family and the cap by
   scanning the whole mat, and `SpawnerSystem.syncAlerts` rescans every spawner's
   status every tick too. Result: **`TimedChanges.tick` is the largest stage of
   the realistic tick (0.80 of 1.45 ms)**. Owner: R1/R3 (`SpawnerSystem.js`,
   `TimedChanges.js:315-327`).
2. **`rebuildAll` is quadratic even without an aura** — `applicableStatements`
   walks every Token for every Token. The aura decides how *often* a whole-mat
   rebuild happens; the cost of one is quadratic regardless.
3. **The realistic board misses the p99 target before any aura** (3.4 vs 1.5 ms)
   — the §4.4 targets may need the owner's second look (Q3), or the first fix
   wave will have to land to meet them.
4. **S3 is a cliff, not a bend**: p50 6 ms but p99 71 ms and max 246 ms.
5. **Player drops into a full area always fly back** in S4 (0 of 50 landed),
   and each refusal still costs ~2 ms — cheap, but it is a full nudge search.

## What the bench cannot measure

Drawing, React, the browser's own JIT/GC, wall-clock behaviour (the tick clamp
and time-bank overflow), and anything the fixtures don't exercise (triggers,
`Cannot` rules, statuses, promotions). Two deliberate differences from the
browser: `window` is undefined, so SpriteLayer skips a `setTimeout` (the tick
does the same absorb on a virtual clock) and AudioSystem skips its unlock
listeners. Push-solver *pair checks* are not counted, only `relax` calls and
time. Full detail in `bench/README.md`.
