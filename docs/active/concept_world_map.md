# The world map: expanding into named places

*Owner workshop, 2026-10-28, the gating question from
[concept_progression_ledger.md](concept_progression_ledger.md). Research:
[the player journey](research/gating_player_journey_2026-10-28.md) and
[how other games gate](research/gating_games_study_2026-10-28.md). Would
replace map items as the gate to new content, and reshape the Atlas
([concept_atlas.md](concept_atlas.md), built A1–A12).*

## The problem (owner)

- Today's map gate is **hidden, misplaced and luck-driven**: guaranteed first-
  kill maps read as "10 %", a map's contents show only once held, the sure
  Volcano map sits on a level-20 boss, and more copies are boss farming at
  3 %.
- Deeper than that: maps **don't give the feeling of expansion** the owner
  wants. The playmat looks and works as it should, but **it lacks a cohesive
  place within the world**. It doesn't feel like discovering something;
  "having an Iron Ore map seems too mechanical."

## What a good gate does (owner)

- **The player sees it coming**, **it feels like discovery**, **it uses many
  skills**.
- It feels like **"Needs Forestry 15"**: an honest, visible goal on the thing
  itself.
- **The whole guild earns it**: several heroes' work together (fits hero
  orders: one hero, one job).

## The picture so far (owner)

- **One world map of named places with a fog frontier.** Settled places are
  lit; their neighbours show as silhouettes at the edge of the fog. You push
  outward from where you are, so iron lives in a real place you saw on the
  horizon.
- **The world is cohesive through**:
  - **geography**: neighbours and distances; where you are decides what you
    can reach next;
  - **names and character**: each place has a name, a look and a line of
    story, not "a Volcano map";
  - **the world changes on the playmat** (owner): the world map itself is
    essentially static; life and change happen on the mat.
- **Each next place shows its requirements** (hover the cell: "To reach the
  Ashen Hills: 30 rations, 2 pickaxes, 20 planks, Mining 12"). **The guild
  meets them, the fog lifts and the place is settled**; the fight on arrival
  (below) is the last step. **No separate gate or pass Tokens** (owner,
  2026-10-29): the requirement and the arrival fight are the gate.
- **The world map is a representation**: cells with a few icons. The playmat
  is where things happen (owner).
- **Map items retire** (owner): places, not items, are the gate.
- **One place, one Region**: settling a place writes its Region on the
  playmat (layout from its biome and character, as the Atlas does today);
  travel between settled places stays free and instant.

## How the world grows (owner)

- **The world map starts with the Guild Hall and builds outward.** It is a
  grid of cells (e.g. hexes) around the Hall; **every fog cell touching
  settled land holds one randomly generated region option**, which stays put
  until explored. Settling a cell reveals the cells beyond it, so where the
  guild goes decides what lies next. Choosing which region to explore is the
  expedition decision.
- **Regions are randomly generated**; a new world is generated for each run
  after the ritual (owner).
- **Tiers by distance from the Hall** (owner): rings outward. The first ring is
  copper-and-oak country, the next iron-and-fir, and so on; requirements rise
  with distance. **The cost of every cell rises with its ring distance, so
  there are always affordable options** nearer in (owner).
- **Connected geography and about 8 landmarks per run** (owner, 2026-10-29):
  the world is generated as shapes (a mountain chain, a coast, a forest
  belt), so neighbours make sense. **A landmark is a special cell that opens
  its tier** (the Smoking Mountain): visible on the horizon from the start,
  with a bigger requirement and a tougher arrival fight (a boss); settling it
  is the tier's milestone and opens the next ring around it.
- **Each ring guarantees its key materials** (owner): the second ring always
  offers iron and fir among its cells. Randomness shapes where and with what,
  never whether. The ledger plans rings as stages and checks the rules.
- **A fog cell shows its core resource outputs** and its requirements
  (materials and skill levels) (owner), so a player who needs a particular
  item can find the region that gives it. Smaller extras (treasures, a rare
  node, modifiers like Rich Veins, a boss) can stay a surprise on arrival
  (planning agent's default).
- **Paying the requirements settles it at once, all at once** (owner): no
  deposits over time, no expedition timer, no heroes away.
- **A newly settled region arrives occupied** (owner): its playmat is
  darkened, background and Tokens, with an encounter on it; **the guild must
  defeat all the enemies before the region unlocks** (its Tokens brighten and
  can be worked). Combat joins gathering and crafting in expansion.
- **The encounter is small and fixed** (owner, 2026-10-29): a handful of
  enemies (3–6) sized to the ring's level, sometimes a boss (always at a
  landmark); they don't respawn. Clearing takes minutes to an hour, not a
  siege. While the player is away, heroes sent at it keep fighting as long as
  they win; a defeated hero waits at the Hall (hero orders).
- **Places the guild has left are frozen**, as today (owner): only the active
  Region runs.

### Rules from the theorycraft (planning agent's defaults; owner may change)

- **Key materials per frontier, not per ring**: whichever way the guild
  expands, the next ring's key materials (iron, fir) are within reach of its
  frontier, never only on the far side.
- **No iron to reach iron**: a ring-N cell asks only for materials of earlier
  rings, plus skill levels.
- **Few trips to pay**: a cell's price can be gathered from one or two settled
  places.
- The ledger checks all three on each generated world's rules.

## What goes on a mat (owner, 2026-10-29)

*From the [mat composition study](research/mat_composition_study_2026-10-29.md).
Goals: a diverse selection of activities on any mat (**every mat supports most
skills, plus a specialty**), discovery from **arriving somewhere new**, and
balance (worries: lucky or unlucky worlds; skills progressing unevenly).*

- **Natural work on arrival keeps about 4–6 heroes busy in total** (owner):
  kit and specialty together. Beyond that, extra work comes from **what the
  player builds** (camps, planted Forests, Mines, Fields on Foundations), so
  arriving is a start and building is the growth; this frees Token cap for
  building.
- **A specialty means better things, not more** (owner): the new-tier node, a
  rare or rich node, its look and camp. (About 5 Tokens keep one hero busy;
  quantity past that is wasted.)
- **Several kinds of kit, so mats feel different** (owner): **kit archetypes**
  (e.g. Riverside, Highland, Woodland, Wetland, Plains; names placeholders),
  each covering most skills in its own balance and look. The other levers
  offered (different Tokens per skill, a local building, a local quirk,
  layout puzzles, local enemies, neighbour edges) were not chosen for now; the
  owner wants to keep brainstorming.
- **Against luck: a specialty bag per ring, a pity rule and a value budget
  per cell** (owner): every gathering skill appears as a specialty in every
  ring; a skill not offered in the last 4 options comes next; a cell's
  modifiers and treasures stay within ±10 % of its ring's value. The ledger
  tests many generated worlds against per-skill bands.
- Found by the study, for the ledger and content: fishing earns ~450 XP/h
  against ~1,100 for mining; most of today's maps are single-resource.

## Explored and set aside (owner, 2026-10-29)

- **Influence and player-placed cells**: a meta-resource (earned from bounties
  and quests) buying packs of 3 cells, keep 1, placed on a blank parchment
  around the Hall, tiers by ring. After discussing it the owner felt **packs
  and placing cells don't quite work**; **the generated world of cells and
  rings stays the best option**. In a generated world **the player can see
  what lies on each cell**, so a player needing a resource can go to it.
- **Tiles into sockets** and the other arrival levers from the
  [arrival study](research/arrival_identity_study_2026-10-29.md) were not
  chosen; **the story site as the arrival** was liked (owner): the arrival
  enemies come from the place's one story site (goblins holed up in the
  mine), its chain uses other skills, ending in restore or strip.

## Built pieces (owner: the recommended clean-up)

- **Retire**: map items, map drops from enemies and bosses, bounty map rewards,
  map upcycling, the tutorial's three maps (the tutorial's end needs a new
  reward: the first ring opening).
- **Keep, inside generation**: region modifiers and treasures, as hidden
  extras of a generated region.
- **The Cartography Table upgrade becomes "see further"**: it reveals the
  options of fog cells further ahead.
- **The Atlas tome becomes the world map screen.**
- Travel between settled places stays free and instant; one place, one
  Region (above).

## Open

- How many cells per ring, the cell shape, and how a cell's requirements are
  priced (the simulator and the ledger's stage budgets).
- How the ledger's stages map onto rings (Starter Camp = the Hall's cell,
  ring 1 = plane 1, ring 2 = plane 2).
- Which enemies make up each ring's encounters, and the landmark bosses
  (content, through the ledger).
- The tutorial's new ending (the first ring opening) and the Starter Camp as
  the Hall's cell.
- Theorycraft and alternatives were weighed 2026-10-28
  ([research](research/world_map_theorycraft_2026-10-28.md)); the owner kept the
  world map, without pass Tokens on the mat.
