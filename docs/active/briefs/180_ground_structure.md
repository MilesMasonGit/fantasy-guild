# Brief 180 — Ground structure: tint pattern, organic earth, grouped props, edge pieces

**Source:** the owner's terrain goal (2026-10-16): the director advises, the owner
draws. Notes with mock-ups: https://claude.ai/artifact/PLTLkP5bqjLqtTc8YYJSpg
(sections 1–9). Target feel: Sea of Stars (owner). Also read the "Terrain art
guide" in [`../research/terrain_art_list.md`](../research/terrain_art_list.md),
[`ART_STYLE.md`](../../reference/ART_STYLE.md) and
[`ASSET_NAMING.md`](../../reference/ASSET_NAMING.md).

**Ruled (owner, 2026-10-16):** edges stay computed by code (L-3 A stands), but
the code lays **hand-drawn edge pieces** along them: grass tufts over earth,
pebbles in seams, wet sand at the waterline. Not full transition tiles (too
labour-intensive). The owner thinks the ground "lacks structure"; the cure is
structure at three sizes: a large tint pattern, medium shapes and groups, small
detail in the sheets (the owner's drawing).

**Why it looks plain today (measured):** one 64 px sheet repeated like carpet;
props sprinkled evenly one per cell; earth patches nearly black (sheet mean
brightness 30) and hard-edged; every ground in the same brightness band as the
Tokens' bodies.

## G1 — Structure in code (engineer; eye-check)

Code under `src/systems/board/Terrain*.js`, `src/config/registries/terrainRegistry.js`,
`src/ui/components/board/TerrainCanvas.jsx`, tuning sliders in the dev panel's
Ground tuner (`playmatTuning`). The plan is built once per Region and on resize,
never per frame: keep it that way (measure the build time before and after).

1. **Tint pattern:** per look, two or three shades of its sheet (one lighter,
   one darker, a few percent each way) in large soft areas several Tokens wide,
   from smooth value noise on a coarse grid, stepped (hard edges between
   shades, pixel-art style, no gradients). Sliders: scale, strength, on/off.
2. **Organic earth with a rim:** worn patches keep their noise shape but get a
   darker rim on the earth side and a lighter fringe on the grass side, each
   one ground pixel or two wide; coverage slider defaults lower (about 10 % of
   a grass Region). Optional, behind a slider: wear more near Tokens and less
   toward the mat's edges ("feet go here").
3. **Grouped props:** scatter in small groups (3–6 of one kind around a point)
   with open grass between, instead of one per cell; density and group size on
   sliders. Props keep every existing rule (own ground, never on beaches cut in,
   never workable-looking).
4. **No change** to the sheets' art or the code dimming in this phase (the
   dimming goes when the owner's new sheets land).

Done when: Starter Camp, a Forest and a Coast Region show all three; tests for
the pure parts (shade map, rim, grouping); the engine work check unchanged; the
ground build time reported before and after; screenshots in colour and grey.

## G3 — Generated ground in the game (engineer)

Move the settled rules of the final prototype into the game's ground plan, so
the grass and earth of meadow and forest looks are **generated per pixel**
instead of sampled from a repeating sheet. Spec: the generator
(`docs/active/research/ground_gen/gen.py`, functions `ground` and `mixed`)
and the parameter set `final_spec.json` (lawn, tall, dry kinds; zone scale and
raggedness). The prototype's Python is the reference for the look; the game's
version must match it by eye on the same parameters, not pixel for pixel.

- Generated, not sampled: body tones, clumps, mounds with lit and shadowed
  edges, blade strokes and tufts (lit from the top-left, shadow pixel), texture
  in patches, kinds of grass in ragged zones sharing one base colour, earth
  with clods, a lighter middle, a sunken edge, grass fingers and tufts into it.
  Palettes as hue-shifted 5-colour ramps per look.
- Keeps G1's tint pattern (one strength), grouped props, every placement rule,
  and determinism per Region seed. The plan stays built once per Region and on
  resize, never per frame; report the build time (budget: well under 100 ms
  per Region on this machine; say if it isn't).
- Sliders in the Ground tuner for the parameters the owner tuned in the rounds
  (stroke density, patch mask, zone scale, earth amount, clump contrast and
  size, mound strength, tall/dry amounts), defaults from `final_spec.json`.
- Other looks (sand, sea, rock, gravel) keep their sheets for now; biome
  variants are the next design rounds, using the same machinery.
- The code dimming of the ground is switched off for the generated looks (the
  palettes are the final colours).

Done when: the Starter Camp and a Forest show the generated ground; side-by-side
screenshots against `final.png`; tests for the pure parts (determinism, earth
mask with encroachment, zone mixing, ramp); engine work check same; a
`bench:draw --compare` for the cost log; eye-check.

## G5 — Water in every Region (engineer; eye-check)

The settled world rounds (page above, rounds W1 and W5–W7; prototypes `world6.py`,
`world7.py` with `gen.py`'s `water_field`). Today `carveWater`
(`src/systems/atlas/TerrainMap.js`) only cuts a sea along one mat edge, on the
32-unit cells, so shores step like stairs.

1. **Water as shapes**, in mat units, generated from the Region's seed: **lakes**
   (several merged lobes, shore bent twice by noise, bays, sometimes an island;
   up to a fifth of the mat), **ponds** and **tarns** (small), **rivers** (a path
   bent by noise along its length, never even curves, widening downstream;
   crossing the mat edge to edge, or from a spring pool to an edge, or into and
   out of a lake), a river's **uneven split** (a wide main arm and a thin side
   channel round a teardrop island; may end in a small delta of two mouths near
   the edge), and the **braided** river (several channels weaving round gravel
   bars, for mountains). **No thin stream joins other water** (owner: those joins
   looked wrong); wide arms rejoining and rivers meeting a lake are fine. The
   Coast's edge sea stays, drawn from the same shape machinery.
2. **Per-biome rules** (owner, W5): meadow and flower meadow a lake with a river
   to the edge; forest and cherry grove a pond; mountain a tarn or a braided
   river; volcano none; Yellowstone its sulphur lakes, which become **real water
   ground** (their light blue look kept); Coast the edge sea. A Region with
   several biomes gets its main biome's water plus a small water in the others'
   ground where it fits. The rules sit beside `BIOME_GROUND` in
   `src/systems/atlas/Budget.js`; a variety is picked by seed (the lake, the
   river, or both). If placing a minor biome's water in its own ground needs the
   placement order in `Layout.js` changed, stop and report the options.
3. **Placement** still works on the cells: a cell is water when the shape covers
   it (choose the rule so no Token is drawn standing in water and no water cell
   looks dry); the Hall clearing stays dry; `MAX_WATER_SHARE` holds; "shore" as
   today. Tokens that need water get placed by the existing rules.
4. **Drawing** follows the shape at ground-pixel resolution, not the cells:
   smooth shores (this also fixes the Coast's staircase), with the water look of
   `water_field` (wet bank, foam line, shallows, deep, waves growing with depth)
   per look in `src/systems/board/TerrainGrass.js`. A beach (sand rim) wherever
   land meets the sea, as today's Coast.
5. **Stored and deterministic**: the shapes (or what regenerates them) live in
   the Region's stored terrain map so a loaded save draws the same; a save
   without them draws as today (old Regions keep their ground; new Regions get
   water). The ground plan stays built once per Region and on resize.
6. Dev panel buttons to roll a Region with each water kind (lake, river across,
   split river, braided, pond, tarn, sulphur lakes, sea).

Done when: Regions of every biome show their water; side-by-sides against

## G2 — Edge pieces (superseded 2026-10-17; kept for reference)

The code lays the owner's edge pieces along every boundary between two grounds
and along worn-patch edges: pieces sit across the boundary line, spaced with
some randomness, never on top of each other, never under a Token's foot. A pair
with no drawn pieces falls back to G1's rim alone.

**Art format (for the owner):** one sheet per pair, 64 × 64, a 4 × 4 grid of
16 px pieces like the prop sheets, named `p_edge_<ground>_<ground>.png` in
`playmat/props/` (e.g. `p_edge_grass_dirt.png`): rows 1–2 overhang tufts or
stones drawn sitting on a horizontal boundary through the cell's middle, row 3
corner clumps, row 4 small seam bits. G2 first makes a template sheet with the
boundary line and the light direction marked, and placeholder pieces cut from
the existing tufts so the placement can be seen before the art exists.

Start with grass↔earth; then grass↔sand and sand↔water (the wet line).
