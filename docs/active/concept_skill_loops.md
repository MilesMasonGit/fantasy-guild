# Skill loops and the board as a puzzle

*D4 of the post-crunch plan ([ideas.md](ideas.md)). Owner interview, started
2026-10-09. **Done** (owner closed it 2026-10-09); specialist skills are
sketches only. Builds on [concept_progression.md](concept_progression.md)
(implicit tiers, chains across skills) and [concept_combat.md](concept_combat.md).*

## Approach

- **One signature mechanic per skill.** Every skill still works Tokens for
  items, but each adds one rule only it has. One twist each keeps the game
  simple and the skills distinct (pillar 3).
- **Layout and adjacency are a main theme across skills.** Many loops reward
  good placement; on the fixed post-Atlas board, arranging the base is the
  main creative act. Players must be able to see it (neighbour effects shown
  while placing, see below).
- **Design the Starter Camp's skills first**: the ones met in the first hour.
  Their loops set the pattern and the Starter Camp and Atlas need them first.
- **Prototype**: sketch every loop's rule here, build the most promising one
  or two quickly in the game (CMS content plus small engine rules), the owner
  plays them, then the rest follow. **Prototyping waits for the Atlas**
  (brief 70), since node behaviour changes there.
- **Seeing adjacency**: **while placing** (holding a Token over the mat lights
  up the Tokens it would help or be helped by, with the effect), **on
  inspect** (a Token lists what boosts it and what it boosts), and **quiet
  pops** when a bonus applies (D1).

- **The rule: gathering skills differ by how their nodes behave** (groves,
  long-burst fields, refilling veins); **station skills differ by what they
  produce** and who needs it (owner, 2026-10-09).
- **Node behaviour is another way skills differ.** Spawners will still be used
  often where they fit (Forests spawn trees); they make less sense for ore
  veins, which refill in place. ⚠️ Amends the Atlas decision "spawners only
  for enemies and lures": player-built growing places spawn too.

- **Gathering tools are hero gear** (owner, 2026-10-09, overturns the GDD's
  "Tools are not hero equipment"): pickaxes, woodaxes, fishing rods, sickles
  are carried **in the hands, alongside weapons** (hands: 2), and set what a
  hero can gather. Tool Tokens on the mat go away. Stations (anvils, furnaces,
  sawmills) stay Tokens on the mat. Tools never wear out (gear never breaks).
- **Tools in detail** (owner interview, 2026-10-20; T-126):
  - **A tool takes one hand**: a tool and a one-handed weapon fit together;
    a two-handed weapon leaves no hand for a tool; two tools (no weapon)
    let a hero do both jobs.
  - **Needed by kind; the tool's tier gates node tiers**: no pickaxe, no
    mining; a Copper Pickaxe mines copper and stone, iron ore needs an Iron
    Pickaxe or better. **Gating only for now** (no speed or yield from the
    tool; numbers later). **Owner 2026-10-20 (supersedes the example): an
    ore needs the pick of the tier below it** — iron is mined with a copper
    pick, mythril with an iron pick, and so on.
  - **No tool**: the hero does other allowed work; the Token they'd work
    shows a **"Needs Pickaxe" speech bubble** (the kind of tool it needs).
  - **Swapping is by hand only**, like weapons, from the Bank.
  - **First tools**: a new game starts with a **Copper** pickaxe, woodaxe
    and sickle and a fishing rod **in the Bank** (owner: no Rusty tier); an
    old save from before tools gets them once on load; the tutorial has a hero equip
    one. Better tools are smithed. New heroes arrive empty-handed.
  - **Content**: tools become **items** (a hand slot and a tier); tool
    Tokens on the mat and recipes that need or spend tool charges go. The
    owner has made a Copper Pickaxe item already; the rest come as a CMS
    change pack for review.

## Loops

### Forestry

- **Signature: groves.** Trees of **the same species** standing together
  **grow (and regrow) faster**: a dense Oak grove beats a lone Oak, and mixed
  woods get no bonus. Forestry is about the shape of the forest. Example
  effect: "increase growth speed for Oak Saplings" on each Oak.
- **Planting belongs to Farming** (see Farming): a Forest Foundation, like
  Farmland for food, grows a Forest that spawns trees.
- **Planted trees become fixtures like the map's own**: they regrow in place
  after felling and count toward the Token cap.
- **Seeds travel**: Fir seeds earned in a Fir Region can be planted anywhere.
  The map is how the guild first reaches a new tree; planting spreads it.
- **Neighbours**: groves, plus **a variety of buildings with different
  effects**, e.g. a **Lumber Camp** that speeds up Forestry work nearby, and a
  **Sawmill**, a Construction or Crafting station that makes planks.
- **Feeds**: Construction (wood, planks, Foundation tiers), Smithing and fuel
  (charcoal, handles), Fletching (bows, arrows).
- **Rare finds**: a few per tree type (a bird's nest, amber, a map fragment on
  old trees), as loud pops.

### Farming

- **Signature: choose what grows, and tend it.** Farming owns **all planting**.
  Growing places are the player's to plan: each is sown with a chosen seed,
  and support Tokens around it help it.
- **Growing places**: **fields** (crops for Cooking), **orchards** (fruit),
  **forests** (timber for Forestry), and **herb gardens, beehives and
  pastures** (herbs for Alchemy, honey and wax, animals).
- **Fields grow long, then burst.** A field grows for **an hour or two** (per
  crop, in the CMS), then bursts out a load of crops. The player leaves it
  alone, then moves a hero's flag over when it's ready:
  - ripe crops **wait forever**, no spoiling; a normal pop says it's ready;
  - **a Farming hero harvests** as normal work (the crops drop as loot) and
    **replants** with seeds from the Bank; then the player moves the flag on.
- **Forests and orchards spawn** instead: they keep putting out trees that are
  worked over time. Each growing place has its own rhythm.
- **Seeds come from**: harvest drops, the Hall's passive production, Region
  finds (a new plant's first seeds come from where it grows), and
  **processing** (making Lemonade from Lemons also drops Lemon Seeds).
- **Neighbours**: Windmill, water, beehives pollinating, perhaps a composter
  that consumes weeds to boost yields. Support Tokens stay loose unless one is
  critical to a skill's identity.

### Mining

- **Overall a simpler skill** (owner): it's being close to some nodes and
  having a decent support building, and that's fine.
- **Signature: rich veins that deplete and refill.** A vein holds a stock of
  ore; mining empties it, then it slowly refills in place. Mining is about
  having enough veins and miners to keep ore flowing. A different rhythm from
  trees (groves, spawning) and fields (long burst).
- **Neighbours**: **minecarts** (today's Copper Ore Minecart restocks a vein,
  generalised to other ores) and **a smelter next door**.
- **Pickaxes are hero gear** (see Approach): the pickaxe in a hero's hand sets
  which ores they can mine (an Iron Pickaxe for Gold).
- **No danger in the mines.** ⚠️ The Atlas concept's example of mining waking a
  rock elemental is dropped for Mining; ambushes live elsewhere.
- **Rare finds**: gems (for crafting, enchanting) and occasionally a map
  fragment, as loud pops.

### Smithing

- **Station skills are distinct by what they produce**, not by a special
  mechanic (owner). Smithing makes **ingots** (smelting at the furnace),
  **gathering tools** (woodaxes, pickaxes: hero gear), **melee gear** (weapons
  and metal armour) and **construction materials** (nails, fittings).
- **Neighbours**: a **furnace and anvil together** work better; otherwise
  Smithing's layout stays simple.
- **Better stations are new buildings**: a Copper Anvil, an Iron Anvil… (the
  D2 "better versions worth building", which fits moving day).
- **Specialised stations**: different station types boost different
  production: a **Smelter** is better at ingots, a **Weapon Forge** at weapons.
  A specialised Region builds the stations its purpose needs.

### Fishing

- **Signature: shifting fish spots.** Water Tokens change what's biting over
  time (as Coast ⇄ Shrimp Coast already does): a spot cycles between catches,
  and a rare fish shows up briefly. Fishing is about being there at the right
  time, gently; offline, heroes catch whatever is biting. **No signal** for a
  rare bite: whoever is fishing there catches it.
- **Fishing rods are hero gear** (see Approach).
- **Neighbours**: a **dock or jetty** at the water **adds a fishing spot**.
- **Feeds**: Cooking (fish dishes, the main fight food), Crafting and
  Enchanting (shells, pearls), Farming (fish as fertiliser).

### Cooking

- **Produces**: **fight food** (healing in fights, combat's main supply) and
  **drinks** (heal from their own slot, T-098, or short buffs). Not chosen:
  work-buff meals, ingredients for other skills.
- **Stations**: **Campfire** (cheap, quick, simple; good on moving day),
  **Kitchen / oven** (better meals; Grandma's Kitchen is one), **Brewery**
  (drinks and beer).

### Crafting

- **The hub skill**: Crafting feeds many skills with diverse items used in many
  places. Crafted String goes into Leather Armour, strung Bows and more. It may
  make some Tokens as well.
- **Produces**: **construction materials** (rope, cloth, glass, bricks),
  **light and fuel** (charcoal, torches), **non-metal gear** (leather and cloth
  armour, trinkets, jewellery), **everyday items** (containers, ceramics,
  wooden parts).
- **Stations**: **Workbench** (general), **Kiln** (ceramics, bricks, glass),
  **Tannery / loom** (leather and cloth).

### Construction

- **Builds a lot of the core buildings and stations** (on Foundations, as
  today: buy a Foundation, choose what to build; Foundation tiers gate higher
  buildings), and demolishes (Atlas).
- **Also builds temporary Tokens** meant to be used up: e.g. a **Guard Tower**
  that prevents enemy spawns nearby until it's exhausted.

### Alchemy

- **Makes magical consumables.** A hero carries them (equipped) and one is
  **used automatically when a work cycle or a fight starts**, applying its
  effect for that cycle. Example: a **Birdwatcher** potion gives a 25 % chance
  of a Bird Nest when a Forestry cycle completes; a player who wants seeds
  during a wood grind brews Birdwatchers and equips them to their woodcutters.
- **Produces**: healing and buff potions for fights (D3), work potions like
  the Birdwatcher, **transmutation** (turning surplus into something useful),
  and **ingredients for magic** (inks, essences for Enchanting).

## Specialist skills — sketches only

The 12 specialist skills (Advanced: Leadership, Fletching, Enchanting, Crime;
Master: Faith, Trapping, Summoning, Taming, Commerce, Science, Armory,
Shadowcraft) are placeholders, and the skill list is open to rework (owner).
Full loops come with the skill list review; sketches are kept here.

- **Enchanting (very loose)**: Tokens need to be **charged with elements**. An
  enchanting table has **three slots** that take context from other placed
  Tokens; charge comes from **leyline map features** or **support Tokens** the
  player builds; **different combinations give different outputs**. Builds on
  the `nearby` reach (164 u, the four side neighbours); leylines would be an
  Atlas map feature or modifier.
- **Fletching**: bows and arrows from Forestry's wood and Crafting's string
  (from the Forestry and Crafting answers).

### Today, as authored (2026-10-22)

An Oak Forest spawns Oak Saplings (allowance 5, every 20 s, one Oak Seedling
each); a Sapling grows into an Oak Tree in 30 s; the Tree has 5 charges and
leaves the mat. A Copper Mine spawns up to 3 Copper Ore Veins, which leave
when spent. A Wheat Field spawns Wheat Sprouts (a seed each) that ripen in
30 s into Ripe Wheat (3 charges). The Coast turns into a Shrimp Coast and
back. The engine already has a **refill** respawn (rest at 0 charges, refill
in place) and a **regrow** respawn (become another Token that grows back);
no content uses either yet.

### Rulings this pass (owner interview, 2026-10-22)

**All gathering skills**
- **Nothing ready in reach: the hero waits silently** and works the first
  Token that becomes ready. No countdown bubble. (Revisit per skill if one
  needs otherwise.)

**Forestry**
- **Felled tree → Stump → Sapling → Tree, in place.** The last log turns the
  Tree into a Stump; the Stump grows into a Sapling, the Sapling into a Tree.
- **Logs per tree: several, per species** in the CMS (Oak about 5 as today).
- **Art: Sapling, Tree and Stump per species** (a Stump per species, not
  shared).
- **A full Oak cycle takes about 5 minutes** (felled to harvestable); each
  species has its own times in the CMS.
- **Forests speed up nearby regrowth**: a Forest Token reduces the grow time
  of Stumps and Saplings within reach. The player plants Forests to help the
  trees around them come back faster.
- **Forests replace groves.** ⚠️ Overturns the 2026-10-09 grove rule: trees no
  longer speed each other up; only Forest Tokens do.
- **A Forest helps its own species only** (an Oak Forest, Oaks): one Forest
  Token per species.
- **A Forest both plants and regrows.** It plants new saplings within reach
  (up to its allowance) and makes felled trees within reach regrow. The
  player either plants Forests for lots of trees to work, or demolishes one
  to make room.
- **Felled with no Forest in reach** (never had one, or it was demolished):
  the Tree becomes a **Stump that does not regrow** until a Forest of its
  species is in reach again. Clearing the stump is a demolish.
- **Map trees**: a wooded Region's map places **wild Forests** among its
  trees, which work exactly like planted ones (and can be demolished).
- **Seeds**: **building a Forest costs seeds**. The Forest has **no upkeep**:
  the **tree pays**. **A Stump spends one seedling from the Bank when it
  regrows into a Sapling**; with none in the Bank the Stump is **stopped**
  and waits. (Easier to understand than a Forest upkeep, owner.)
- **Forests stack fully**, down to a **floor**: a tree never regrows faster
  than a set fraction of its normal time (about a quarter; the number in the
  CMS).
