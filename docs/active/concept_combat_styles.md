# Combat styles: what each one is for

*From the Systems Audit, A15 Melee ([card](audit/A15_melee.md)), owner
interview 2026-11-20. Melee is written here; Ranged, Magic and Stealth are
filled in by their own audit sessions (A16 to A18), then the planning agent
finishes it with the owner. Tickets already filed: T-565 to T-571.*

## The idea

Each combat style is a different way to play, not just a different side of
the matchup cycle. The styles differ in **what they need** to work, **how
steady** their damage is, and **how far gear can specialise** them.

## Melee: the steady, self-sufficient style

In the owner's words (2026-11-20): "Melee's main appeal is that it is
consistent damage and defense without needing a lot of support. Other styles
require ammo like arrows, niche utility items for magic, or are more wild and
inconsistent. Melee can be performed with weapons and armor that don't break
and can all be crafted from the same base metal material, making it a
reliable beginner combat style."

- **Needs nothing else**: no ammo, no consumable kit. Weapons and armour come
  from one metal line (Smithing) and last.
- **Steady**: its steadiness comes from that gear and from small weapon
  traits, not from a special Melee rule in the fight (no built-in miss or
  damage bonus).
- **Defends well**: metal armour and shields cost Melee nothing (they cut
  Magic and Stealth damage today).
- **The beginner style**: barehanded is Melee, and a copper sword is enough
  to start.
- **Weapon kinds**: every kind (sword, greatsword, cleaver, later more)
  carries a small trait that fits it (T-565). Two hands out-damage one hand
  of the same metal, paying for the lost shield (T-568).
- **Levels by metal**: a weapon asks a level in its style by its metal tier,
  copper 1, iron 15, silver 30 and up (T-566).

## Magic: specialised through many items (owner's first note)

"Combat styles like Magic can be specialized through equipment, to be more
efficient by taking advantages of weaknesses and elemental damage, but it
requires a more diverse collection of items, and it takes effort to get all
of those." To be worked out in A17.

## Shared by all four

- The matchup cycle (melee > ranged > magic > stealth > melee) stays; enemy
  styles are spread so every hero style meets good and bad matchups (T-567).
- Each style's text on the hero sheet says what the style is about and the
  weapons that exist, promising nothing (T-569).
- The weapon tier table (damage by metal and speed, level by tier, a
  two-hand line) is the content agent's authoring guide in
  CONTENT_PHILOSOPHY, not code (T-568).
