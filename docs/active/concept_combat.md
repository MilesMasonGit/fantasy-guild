# Combat and gear

*D3 of the post-crunch plan ([ideas.md](ideas.md)). Owner interview,
2026-10-09. **Closed for now (owner, 2026-10-09): general direction only**;
the details are deferred to a later design pass. Today's rules are in GDD §6.*

## Direction

- **Keep it simple.** Today it's "a glorified slap fight"; the rework makes it
  worth caring about without making it complicated.
- **Low numbers**, so one or two extra damage is a noticeable boost.
- **Hearts are HP, displayed simply**: one heart icon with the numbers
  ("♥ 13/20"). There isn't room for a row of hearts.
- **Max HP grows with combat level.**
- **A little randomness**: occasional misses or crits for texture; fights stay
  mostly predictable, so a player can tell whether one is winnable.
- **Prepare, then watch**: fights are automatic, as now. The player's skill is
  preparation: who goes, what gear, which food, where the flag is. Fits idle
  play and offline fights.
- **Combat is an equal pillar** with gathering and crafting: it feeds them
  (drops, maps) and is fed by them (gear, food).
- **Each combat style has one clear trait**, beyond the matchup cycle (for
  example melee holds enemies' attention, ranged hits from a distance, magic
  hits several, stealth strikes first; examples, not decisions). Simple.
- **Status effects come from the Rules system**: lots of them will be built
  there, probably recreating the seven old ones (Poison, Burning, Bleed, Stun,
  Armor Shield, Well Fed, Cookout). The old status engine
  (`StatusEffectSystem.js`, `statusRegistry.js`) is a clean-out candidate;
  check what the Rules system still borrows from it first (its 5-second tick).
- **Each plane brings its own enemies**: wolves in forests, rock elementals
  ambushing miners, stranger things on late planes, each dropping that place's
  materials and maps. The roster grows with the Atlas.
- **Everyone fights.** ⚠️ Today Recruits can't fight. Change: **Recruits get
  a weak basic attack** from the start; promotion gives the real combat skill
  and style. Ambushed gatherers defend themselves, even in the Starter Camp.
- **Bosses fight when the player chooses**: they wait in a lair (or are
  summoned with an item) and only fight when heroes are sent; no boss
  ambushes a sleeping guild.
- **Party fights use flags on the same target**: several heroes' flags on or
  around a boss and they fight together; the "one hero per Token" rule bends
  for bosses. Classes matter through their traits. No party system.
- **Offline danger is only what the player set up**: ambushes and farmed
  enemies happen offline as configured; a badly prepared guild comes back
  bandaged, never ruined.
- **Gear is crafted, plus rare drops**: mostly made by the guild's crafting
  skills from the planes' materials, each piece a clear step up; a few special
  pieces drop from bosses.
- **Healing**: heroes eat equipped food when low in a fight and regenerate
  slowly otherwise, as today. Food (Cooking) is combat's main input.
- **Fights show always-visible HP bars** on the fighters while in combat
  (an exception to D1's quiet-pop default, chosen for clarity).
- **Farming enemies**: **enemy camps** generated by the map (static spawners,
  forever), **lure buildings** with item upkeep, and **ambushes** from working
  certain nodes. Not chosen: summoning items for ordinary enemies.
- **Combat challenges beyond bosses**: **guardians of resources** (a tough
  enemy on a rich node or treasure; beat it to work the node).
- **Roles are loose, through style traits**: melee naturally holds attention,
  magic hits groups, some master classes support (a Paladin heals). Party
  fights reward a mix, no strict tank/healer/damage trinity.
- **Potions are Alchemy's combat supplies**: healing and short buffs heroes use
  automatically in fights, like food. Gives Alchemy (no content yet) a clear
  loop and combat a second supply.
- **Defeat is a bump, not a tragedy** (D1): knocked out, limping home,
  bandaged in the Hall.

## From D2 (progression)

- **Combat is a gate**: a guardian, or an enemy's drop, can stand between the
  guild and a new resource or map.
- **Maps and modifiers drop steadily from enemies the player can farm safely.**
- **Endgame combat challenges**: **bosses per combat style** (each best beaten
  by one style, so a guild needs variety) and **party fights** (bosses that
  need several heroes at once, tied to classes).
- **Drains**: food and potions are used up in fights; **gear never breaks**.

## Deferred to the later pass

HP range, where damage comes from (weapon, level), what armour does, attack
speeds, the four-style cycle's numbers, each style's trait, which statuses,
the enemy roster, gear slots and crafting.

### What the code does today (checked 2026-10-22)

Differs from GDD §6 in places; the code wins:

- Max HP 50 at level 1 on an exponential curve; it is set only when a hero is
  made or loaded, never recomputed on level-up.
- Damage 4 × 1.045^(level−1) + weapon, ±15 % spread; hit 75 % ± skill gap,
  clamped 5–95; block is folded into the miss roll; no crits.
- Fixed swing timers: hero 2.5 s, enemy 3 s. Weapons don't change speed.
- One hero per enemy: a second hero arriving restarts the fight.
- **Recruits already fight** at level 1 (they can be ambushed) but earn no
  combat XP; GDD §4 says they can't.
- No weapons or armour exist in `items.json`; potions are never drunk
  (`consumeLoopConsumables` has no caller); no content applies a status.
- Enemies have no armour field; 4 ship, all melee.
- Fight state lives in memory only and is never saved.

## Detail pass rulings (owner, 2026-10-22)

### Numbers and scaling

- **HP: 10 + combat level** (RuneScape-like): a Recruit has about 10 HP, a
  level-99 hero about 110. Max HP follows the level as it rises.
- **Damage comes mostly from the weapon**: a bronze sword hits about 2, late
  weapons about 8. Level adds a little (about +1 per 20 levels) plus
  accuracy. Getting stronger means crafting the next weapon.
- **Armour blocks flat damage**: each point takes 1 off every hit; a hit
  always does at least 1.
- **The weapon sets attack speed**, in three steps: fast 2 s (daggers,
  wands), normal 3 s (swords, bows), slow 4 s (two-handers, hitting harder).
- **Fixed hits, rare misses and crits**: a weapon always hits for its number;
  about 1 swing in 10 misses, about 1 in 20 crits for double, each shown
  ("Miss", "Crit!"). No damage spread.
- **An even fight is a duel: 10+ swings, 30 s or more** (a hero against an
  enemy of its tier). Supplies matter. ⚠️ Longer fights mean more steps in
  offline catch-up; check against the envelope (GDD §14) when briefing.
- **Recruits punch for 1** every 3 s, barehanded: no weapons, no style, no
  combat XP. Promotion is how a hero really fights.
- **Weapons belong to styles; a hero uses only their own style's weapons.**
  Some weapons carry more than one style (a Dagger is Melee and Stealth, so
  a Fighter or a Rogue can use it).
- **No regen in fights**: mid-fight, only food and potions heal. Outside
  fights about 1 HP every 10 s.
- **Enemy numbers: the level fills them, the CMS overrides.** The author
  picks a level, the CMS fills HP, hit, armour and speed for that tier, and
  any of them can be changed per enemy. Enemies gain an armour field.
- **Level gap matters a little, through misses**: base miss 10 %, moving
  between about 5 % and 25 % with the gap, plus the small damage step.
- **Defeat keeps today's cost** (amends D2's "gear never breaks"): 25 % of
  each carried food and drink stack lost, a 10 % chance per gear piece to be
  lost, wounded 5 minutes, back at half HP. Gear still never wears out; it
  can only be lost on a knockout.

### The four styles

- **Styles are told apart by the matchup cycle and their weapons, with no
  traits of their own** (amends "each combat style has one clear trait"
  above). Special abilities come later from master classes (a Paladin
  heals) through the Rules system.
- **The cycle stays**: melee > ranged > magic > stealth > melee; melee/magic
  and ranged/stealth are even.
- **A good matchup is worth about +25 % damage** (the bad one −25 %). With
  small early numbers it barely shows, which is intended: the cycle starts
  to matter past the early game.
- **The player sees only the enemy's style icon** and learns the cycle; no
  "weak to" line or matchup arrow.

### Food and potions in fights

- **Eating stays as today**: below 25 % HP the hero eats equipped food;
  eating takes that swing. Food is the only heal in a fight.
- **Drinks stop healing and become small buffs** (amends T-098's "drinks
  heal from their own slot"). ⚠️ Overlap: drinks are Cooking's; their buffs
  are for the skill-loops planning agent to shape.
- **An elixir is drunk at the start of every fight**; each equipped elixir
  type is drunk once, and their effects stack. Some elixirs are for skills
  instead and fire when the hero starts working a Token (the Birdwatcher in
  `concept_skill_loops.md`; overlap with the skill-loops agent).
- **Elixirs need a wide effect library**: +damage, +armour, faster swings,
  fewer misses or more crits to start, and later many more (better loot,
  slowing the enemy…). So an elixir is a Rules-system effect ("When a fight
  starts, provides … to the hero for the fight"), not a fixed stat list.
- **Combat potions are elixirs only**: drunk automatically when a fight
  starts, lasting that fight. Cooking's food heals, Alchemy buffs. No healing
  potions (amends "Potions are Alchemy's combat supplies: healing and short
  buffs" above).
- **A "used" bubble for every item**: when a hero eats, drinks or drinks an
  elixir, a small bubble over them shows the item's icon and what it did
  ("+5 ♥", "Valor") for about 1.5 s.
