# Combat and gear

*D3 of the post-crunch plan ([ideas.md](ideas.md)). Owner interview,
2026-10-09. **Closed for now (owner, 2026-10-09): general direction only**;
the details are deferred to a later design pass. Today's rules are in GDD §6.*

## Direction

- **Keep it simple.** Today it's "a glorified slap fight"; the rework makes it
  worth caring about without making it complicated.
- **Low numbers**, so one or two extra damage is a noticeable boost.
- **Hearts are HP, displayed simply**: one heart icon with the numbers
  ("♥ 13/20"). There isn't room for a row of hearts.
- **Max HP grows with combat level.**
- **A little randomness**: occasional misses or crits for texture; fights stay
  mostly predictable, so a player can tell whether one is winnable.
- **Prepare, then watch**: fights are automatic, as now. The player's skill is
  preparation: who goes, what gear, which food, where the flag is. Fits idle
  play and offline fights.
- **Combat is an equal pillar** with gathering and crafting: it feeds them
  (drops, maps) and is fed by them (gear, food).
- **Each combat style has one clear trait**, beyond the matchup cycle (for
  example melee holds enemies' attention, ranged hits from a distance, magic
  hits several, stealth strikes first; examples, not decisions). Simple.
- **Status effects come from the Rules system**: lots of them will be built
  there, probably recreating the seven old ones (Poison, Burning, Bleed, Stun,
  Armor Shield, Well Fed, Cookout). The old status engine
  (`StatusEffectSystem.js`, `statusRegistry.js`) is a clean-out candidate;
  check what the Rules system still borrows from it first (its 5-second tick).
- **Each plane brings its own enemies**: wolves in forests, rock elementals
  ambushing miners, stranger things on late planes, each dropping that place's
  materials and maps. The roster grows with the Atlas.
- **Everyone fights.** ⚠️ Today Recruits can't fight. Change: **Recruits get
  a weak basic attack** from the start; promotion gives the real combat skill
  and style. Ambushed gatherers defend themselves, even in the Starter Camp.
- **Bosses fight when the player chooses**: they wait in a lair (or are
  summoned with an item) and only fight when heroes are sent; no boss
  ambushes a sleeping guild.
- **Party fights use flags on the same target**: several heroes' flags on or
  around a boss and they fight together; the "one hero per Token" rule bends
  for bosses. Classes matter through their traits. No party system.
- **Offline danger is only what the player set up**: ambushes and farmed
  enemies happen offline as configured; a badly prepared guild comes back
  bandaged, never ruined.
- **Gear is crafted, plus rare drops**: mostly made by the guild's crafting
  skills from the planes' materials, each piece a clear step up; a few special
  pieces drop from bosses.
- **Healing**: heroes eat equipped food when low in a fight and regenerate
  slowly otherwise, as today. Food (Cooking) is combat's main input.
- **Fights show always-visible HP bars** on the fighters while in combat
  (an exception to D1's quiet-pop default, chosen for clarity).
- **Farming enemies**: **enemy camps** generated by the map (static spawners,
  forever), **lure buildings** with item upkeep, and **ambushes** from working
  certain nodes. Not chosen: summoning items for ordinary enemies.
- **Combat challenges beyond bosses**: **guardians of resources** (a tough
  enemy on a rich node or treasure; beat it to work the node).
- **Roles are loose, through style traits**: melee naturally holds attention,
  magic hits groups, some master classes support (a Paladin heals). Party
  fights reward a mix, no strict tank/healer/damage trinity.
- **Potions are Alchemy's combat supplies**: healing and short buffs heroes use
  automatically in fights, like food. Gives Alchemy (no content yet) a clear
  loop and combat a second supply.
- **Defeat is a bump, not a tragedy** (D1): knocked out, limping home,
  bandaged in the Hall.

## From D2 (progression)

- **Combat is a gate**: a guardian, or an enemy's drop, can stand between the
  guild and a new resource or map.
- **Maps and modifiers drop steadily from enemies the player can farm safely.**
- **Endgame combat challenges**: **bosses per combat style** (each best beaten
  by one style, so a guild needs variety) and **party fights** (bosses that
  need several heroes at once, tied to classes).
- **Drains**: food and potions are used up in fights; **gear never breaks**.

## Deferred to the later pass

HP range, where damage comes from (weapon, level), what armour does, attack
speeds, the four-style cycle's numbers, each style's trait, which statuses,
the enemy roster, gear slots and crafting.

### What the code does today (checked 2026-10-22)

Differs from GDD §6 in places; the code wins:

- Max HP 50 at level 1 on an exponential curve; it is set only when a hero is
  made or loaded, never recomputed on level-up.
- Damage 4 × 1.045^(level−1) + weapon, ±15 % spread; hit 75 % ± skill gap,
  clamped 5–95; block is folded into the miss roll; no crits.
- Fixed swing timers: hero 2.5 s, enemy 3 s. Weapons don't change speed.
- One hero per enemy: a second hero arriving restarts the fight.
- **Recruits already fight** at level 1 (they can be ambushed) but earn no
  combat XP; GDD §4 says they can't.
- No weapons or armour exist in `items.json`; potions are never drunk
  (`consumeLoopConsumables` has no caller); no content applies a status.
- Enemies have no armour field; 4 ship, all melee.
- Fight state lives in memory only and is never saved.

*C1 (2026-10-24) replaced the numbers above; see "The level table and weapon
tiers" at the end. Checked then: max HP was already recomputed on level-up
(`SkillSystem.addXP` calls `updateHeroFromSkills`) and on load.*

## Detail pass rulings (owner, 2026-10-22)

### Numbers and scaling

- **HP: 10 + combat level** (RuneScape-like): a Recruit has about 10 HP, a
  level-99 hero about 110. Max HP follows the level as it rises.
- **Damage comes mostly from the weapon**: a bronze sword hits about 2, late
  weapons about 8. Level adds a little (about +1 per 20 levels) plus
  accuracy. Getting stronger means crafting the next weapon.
- **Armour blocks flat damage**: each point takes 1 off every hit; a hit
  always does at least 1.
- **The weapon sets attack speed**, in three steps: fast 2 s (daggers,
  wands), normal 3 s (swords, bows), slow 4 s (two-handers, hitting harder).
- **Fixed hits, rare misses and crits**: a weapon always hits for its number;
  about 1 swing in 10 misses, about 1 in 20 crits for double, each shown
  ("Miss", "Crit!"). No damage spread.
- **An even fight is a duel: 10+ swings, 30 s or more** (a hero against an
  enemy of its tier). Supplies matter. ⚠️ Longer fights mean more steps in
  offline catch-up; check against the envelope (GDD §14) when briefing.
- **Recruits punch for 1** every 3 s, barehanded: no weapons, no style, no
  combat XP. Promotion is how a hero really fights.
- **Weapons belong to styles; a hero uses only their own style's weapons.**
  Some weapons carry more than one style (a Dagger is Melee and Stealth, so
  a Fighter or a Rogue can use it).
- **No regen in fights**: mid-fight, only food and potions heal. Outside
  fights about 1 HP every 10 s.
- **Enemy numbers: the level fills them, the CMS overrides.** The author
  picks a level, the CMS fills HP, hit, armour and speed for that tier, and
  any of them can be changed per enemy. Enemies gain an armour field.
- **Level gap matters a little, through misses**: base miss 10 %, moving
  between about 5 % and 25 % with the gap, plus the small damage step.
- **Defeat keeps today's cost** (amends D2's "gear never breaks"): 25 % of
  each carried food and drink stack lost, a 10 % chance per gear piece to be
  lost, wounded 5 minutes, back at half HP. Gear still never wears out; it
  can only be lost on a knockout. Confirmed after review (2026-10-24): the
  25 % is of the **whole Bank stack** of each carried food, as the code does,
  and gear is destroyed, a gatherer's only pickaxe included. Deliberate.

### The four styles

- **Styles are told apart by the matchup cycle and their weapons, with no
  traits of their own** (amends "each combat style has one clear trait"
  above). Special abilities come later from master classes (a Paladin
  heals) through the Rules system.
- **The cycle stays**: melee > ranged > magic > stealth > melee; melee/magic
  and ranged/stealth are even.
- **A good matchup is worth about +25 % damage** (the bad one −25 %). With
  small early numbers it barely shows, which is intended: the cycle starts
  to matter past the early game.
- **The player sees only the enemy's style icon** and learns the cycle; no
  "weak to" line or matchup arrow.

### Food and potions in fights

- **Eating stays as today**: below 25 % HP the hero eats equipped food;
  eating takes that swing. Food is the only heal in a fight. Deliberate
  (confirmed after review): a hero can be knocked out with food left, so an
  under-levelled hero can't win an out-levelled fight on food alone.
- **Drinks stop healing and become small buffs** (amends T-098's "drinks
  heal from their own slot"). ⚠️ Overlap: drinks are Cooking's; their buffs
  are for the skill-loops planning agent to shape.
- **An elixir is drunk at the start of a fight and lasts 10 fights**
  (owner after review; the same 10 for every elixir, never per item). Each
  equipped elixir type is drunk when the last one runs out; their effects
  stack. Some elixirs are for skills
  instead and fire when the hero starts working a Token (the Birdwatcher in
  `concept_skill_loops.md`; overlap with the skill-loops agent).
- **Elixirs need a wide effect library**: +damage, +armour, faster swings,
  fewer misses or more crits to start, and later many more (better loot,
  slowing the enemy…). So an elixir is a Rules-system effect ("When a fight
  starts, provides … to the hero for the fight"), not a fixed stat list.

### Encounters

- **Every fight is one-on-one**, bosses and guardians included (amends
  "Party fights use flags on the same target" and the party-fight bosses of
  D2 above). **Party fights are parked as a later idea**, not dropped. A
  second hero flagged at a busy enemy waits or looks for another enemy; it
  no longer restarts the fight.
- **A boss heals to full between attempts**: if its hero is knocked out, the
  next flagged hero starts a clean duel.
- **A beaten guardian returns after a while** and must be beaten again
  before the node can be worked; it drops again each time.
- **Node ambushes are a chance per work cycle**: a Token can carry an ambush
  rule (each finished cycle has a small chance, about 5 %, to spawn its local
  enemy, which attacks the worker), set per Token in the CMS. The engine's
  rule-spawned enemy with a firing chance is the starting point.
- **Camps last forever**, as today: a safe, steady farm.
- **Bosses come from lairs and summon items**: most wait in a lair Token
  that comes with a map; some are summoned with an item. Bosses never ambush.
- **Bosses return on a short timer** and can be fought over and over to farm
  their rare drops; a summoned boss is fought again with another summon item.
- **Lure buildings: later** (parked, not in this pass).
- **Bosses are fought offline** like everything else, if flags are set.
- **Bosses have a style plus special abilities and attacks** of their own,
  **made as Rules-system moves in the CMS**, per boss. First moves: a
  **heavy strike** every few swings, a **status attack** (poison or burn),
  **heal or shield** (once, or at low HP; a boss "shield" is simply
  "gains +2 armour", no Shield status needed), and **enrage** below a share
  of HP (faster or harder hits). This needs new moments such as "every Nth
  swing" and "when HP falls below X".

### Status effects

- **Built in the Rules system; first two: Poison and Burn.** Bleed, Stun
  and Shield wait for a later pass. Well Fed and Cookout were food buffs;
  with drinks becoming buffs they go to the skill-loops agent (overlap).
- **Damage over time is 1 per tick, about every 3 s** (a normal swing's
  pace); stacks or duration make it stronger.
- **Statuses end with the fight.** No hero falls to poison on the way home.
- **Poison stacks, up to 3**: each poisoning adds a stack, 1 damage per
  stack per tick, until the fight ends. **Burn** doesn't stack: 1 per tick for a few
  ticks, and food heals half while burning.
- The old status engine (`StatusEffectSystem.js`, `statusRegistry.js`) is
  retired once nothing borrows from it.

### Loot and XP

- **Kill XP: the level fills it, the CMS overrides**, like enemy stats. XP
  goes to the hero's one combat skill; Recruits earn none.
- **Maps sit behind skill gates** (owner, review round 1; replaces an
  earlier "maps only from bosses and guardians"). "Boss" and "guardian" are
  not the point: the next tier of maps drops from an enemy the guild must
  be ready for. Combat gates are the most common; other skills may gate
  content too. Example: a well-equipped combat-20 hero is needed to beat the
  Goblin Chief, which drops the Iron Ore maps; to get there the player
  crafts copper weapons, levels the hero on weaker goblins, and makes food
  and elixirs. Which enemy drops which map stays the basic-content agent's
  (`concept_basic_content.md`), whose plan (goblins drop plane-1 maps now
  and then) stands.

### Depth (options in [research/combat_depth_options.md](research/combat_depth_options.md))

- **Miss chance = 10 % ± level gap + the enemy's Dodge − the hero's
  Accuracy**, kept between 5 % and 40 % (extends the level-gap ruling).
- **Pierce**: a weapon stat that ignores that many armour points (armour
  stays flat, minimum 1 per hit).
- **Crits double before armour** (iron sword 4 against armour 2: a normal hit
  does 2, a crit 8 − 2 = 6), and gear can raise crit chance.
- **Damage stays fixed per weapon.** Weapon traits ("every 4th swing +1")
  are a later idea, built on the bosses' "every Nth swing" moment.
- **Gear carries a main stat plus a special ability**, more than a plain
  number: e.g. a **Cleaver** is a melee weapon hitting a little less than a
  sword but with a chance to double Meat drops. Abilities are Rules-system
  effects on the item.
- **The ability comes from the weapon family**: every Cleaver doubles meat
  sometimes, every Spear pierces; each tier (bronze, iron) keeps the ability
  and raises its number.
- **Stat bonuses are abilities too**: a family's ability may be a stat
  (Spear: Pierce 1, Bow: +Accuracy, Rapier: +Crit; Vitality, Guard) or
  something special (Cleaver: more meat). One idea, "what this piece is good
  at", authored in the Rules editor.
- **Armour families get abilities the same way** (a Hide Vest: poison stacks
  at most 1 lower). Armour sets are a later idea.
- **An ability can touch anything**, work and gathering included (a combat
  weapon could also help a work skill). ⚠️ Overlap: tools are the
  skill-loops agent's; gear that boosts work must fit their loops.
- **An enemy card with numbers**: hovering an enemy (and the combat panel)
  shows its level, style icon, HP, hit, armour and speed; its kinds
  (Armoured, Nimble…) appear once the guild has fought one. No forecast:
  the player does the sums.
- **No resting before fights**: heroes fight at whatever HP they have; in an
  emergency the player moves the hero out by hand.
- **No fight preview.** The player learns which fights are winnable by
  playing and from the combat panel (amends "a player can tell whether one
  is winnable" in the direction: readability comes from small fixed numbers,
  not a forecast).
- **No hero tactics settings.** The player's choices are equipment,
  consumables, and which enemy to fight by placing the hero's flag.
- **An overnight combat recap**: one combat block in the offline catch-up
  summary (fights won and lost, food and elixirs used, drops, knockouts).
- **Gear slots stay as built**: two hands (a tool takes one, a two-handed
  weapon both), hat, chest, two trinkets, food, drink, unlimited
  consumables (elixirs).
- **Boss gear is fixed**, authored per piece; no random rolls anywhere.
- **Enemy kinds exist, without icons**: CMS presets the player discovers in
  the combat panel or by losing. All five from the start: **Armoured** (+2
  armour, slow), **Nimble** (Dodge 15), **Swift** (small hits every 2 s),
  **Brute** (big hits every 4 s), **Venomous** (poisons on hit). An enemy has
  none, one or two.

### The combat UI (built after the rest)

- **Floating text for every hit, both ways**: crisp pixel numbers for damage
  dealt and taken (white on the enemy, red on the hero), "Miss", a bigger
  "Crit!", green heals, tick numbers for poison and burn.
- **The hero-to-enemy link is the bubble row** of brief 210 F2 (T-239):
  while fighting, the row between them shows the two style icons and the
  active elixirs. No tether line.
- **A combat panel shows everything happening in a fight**: a small combat
  icon appears above each fight; clicking it opens the panel (both
  fighters' HP, statuses with stacks, elixirs and buffs, styles, what just
  happened). Statuses are not drawn on the board. **The panel opens over
  the playmat, like the Token inspection popup** (owner 2026-10-24).
- **On the board, without the panel**: HP bars, floating damage and miss
  numbers, and **floating effect bubbles** that pop up briefly when an
  ability or item activates (the "used" bubble above).
- **HP bars carry the number and the percentage** ("8 · 50%").
- **Mock-ups first**: the builder lays out two or three options on the
  style-sheet page, the owner picks from screenshots.
- **Combat potions are elixirs only**: drunk automatically when a fight
  starts, lasting that fight. Cooking's food heals, Alchemy buffs. No healing
  potions (amends "Potions are Alchemy's combat supplies: healing and short
  buffs" above).
- **A "used" bubble for every item**: when a hero eats, drinks or drinks an
  elixir, a small bubble over them shows the item's icon and what it did
  ("+5 ♥", "Valor") for about 1.5 s.

### Planner's calls (routine, not owner rulings)

- **Rounding**: every percentage (matchup ±25 %, Burn's half heal) rounds to
  the nearest whole number, halves up; a hit always does at least 1.
- **The enemy level table and weapon tiers** are proposed by the builder in
  the first phase as one table (level → HP, hit, armour, speed, XP; tier →
  weapon damage), tuned so an even fight lasts 10+ swings; the owner tunes
  it in play.
- **Performance**: the cost of long duels is how many fights run at once,
  measured against GDD §14, not the swings per fight.

### Overlaps with the other planning agents

- **Skill loops** (`concept_skill_loops.md`): drinks become small buffs
  (their shape is theirs); Well Fed and Cookout; elixirs that fire when work
  starts (the Birdwatcher); gear abilities that boost work; tools stay hand
  gear. ⚠️ Their Cooking and Alchemy lines still say drinks and potions heal
  in fights ("heal from their own slot", "healing and buff potions for
  fights"); combat now says only food heals in fights.
- **Basic content** (`concept_basic_content.md`): which enemy drops which map
  (skill gates, above), the enemy roster per plane, and the gear and elixir
  items themselves.

## The level table and weapon tiers (built in C1, 2026-10-24; for the owner to tune)

**Weapon tiers**, following the smithing ingots (copper is the first metal,
`concept_basic_content.md`; "bronze" in the rulings reads as copper). A normal
weapon's damage, a fast one about 2/3 of it and a slow one about 4/3, so the
three deal about the same per second before armour: slow weapons shrug off
armour better, fast ones crit and eat sooner (eating costs one swing).

| Tier | From level | Fast (2 s) | Normal (3 s) | Slow (4 s) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Copper | 1 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
| Iron | 15 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Silver | 30 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
| Gold | 45 | 3 | 5 | 7 |
| Mythril | 60 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| Adamantine | 75 | 5 | 7 | 9 |
| Darkmetal | 90 | 5 | 8 | 11 |

⚠️ A copper fast weapon (1) is the weakest weapon in the game: at tier 1 the
2/3 rounds down to a punch. A dagger family ability (C2) can make up for it.

**Even fights** (a hero with their tier's weapon against an enemy of their own
level, no armour, no food unless said), played by the real engine, 300 duels
each:

| Duel | Sums | Swings | Seconds | Hero wins | HP left (wins) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Level 1, copper sword (2) vs level 1 | 21 HP ÷ 2 = 11 hits; 10 % misses both ways; the enemy lands about 10 × 1 of the hero's 11 HP | 10.9 | 33 | 59 % | 18 % |
| the same with 2 Shrimp (+5) | eats once below 25 % (losing that swing) | 11.7 | 38 | 100 % | 41 % |
| Level 20, iron sword (3 + 1 level step = 4) vs level 20 | 40 ÷ 4 = 10 hits; the enemy lands about 9.5 × 2 of 30 HP | 10.7 | 32 | 100 % | 39 % |
| Level 20, iron dagger (2 + 1 = 3, fast) | 14 hits at 2 s | 14.8 | 30 | 100 % | 41 % |
| Level 20, iron maul (4 + 1 = 5, slow) | 8 hits at 4 s | 8.4 | 34 | 99 % | 35 % |
| Level 50, gold sword (5 + 2 = 7) vs level 50 (armour 1) | 70 ÷ (7 − 1) = 12 hits; the enemy lands about 12 × 3 of 60 HP | 12.8 | 38 | 100 % | 44 % |

Every level from 1 to 99 needs 10 or more swings on average with a normal
weapon (a test checks the sums at every level); the longest even fights are
at the top of a tier (level 14 with copper: by the same sums about 18 swings, 54 s), which is
the push to craft the next tier. Crits double before armour: an iron sword 4
against armour 2 does 2, a crit 8 − 2 = 6.

⚠️ **Level 1 is the hard case**: an 11 HP hero against a 1-damage enemy for
30 s takes about 10 damage, so an unfed level 1 hero wins an even fight only
about 60 % of the time. That follows from three owner numbers together (HP
10 + level, a hit of at least 1, 30 s+ duels); the levers are a shorter fight
or a slower enemy at the lowest levels. Food fixes it today.

**Choices made in C1 the rulings did not spell out** (each a one-number
change):

- **A Recruit is combat level 0** (10 HP); the miss gap reads them at 0 too.
- **A promoted hero with no weapon punches** for 1 + the level step, on their
  own style, every 3 s, and earns XP.
- **The miss slope is 1 point per level** of gap: 5 levels above an enemy
  reaches the 5 % floor, 15 below reaches 25 %.
- **Enemies crit too**, at 5 % unless authored, the same way as heroes.
- **Block is gone** (it was folded into the miss roll and offered in the Rules
  editor; nothing authored used it). Dodge, Pierce and Crit Chance are read
  from gear's Rules effects but not offered in the editor until C2.
- **Older content's `budgetScale`** (the Goblin ×0.3, the Goblin Chief ×0.6)
  still scales the table's HP, hit and XP, so today's goblins stay tutorial
  pushovers until C0 and C2 give them their own numbers.

**Owner rulings on the C1 choices (2026-10-24, by phone):**

- **The Goblin Chief stays as it now is**: it beats a Recruit about 98 times
  in 100. Its purpose is to make the player craft basic equipment to beat
  it.
- **The level-1 even fight stays near a coin toss unfed** (59 %); food fixes
  it. No slower or weaker low-level enemies.
- **A promoted hero with no weapon punches** for 1 + the level step, on their
  own style, and earns combat XP (as built).
- **Block is removed** (as built).
- Director's calls, not asked: a Recruit is combat level 0; the miss slope is
  1 point per level; enemies crit at 5 % unless authored.

## Gear and enemy fields (built in C2, 2026-10-24; for the C0 author)

*What the CMS writes and the game reads. Nothing in `data/` uses them yet.*

**A weapon** (an item in the Hand category with no Tool kind):

| Field | Type | Meaning | Default when absent |
|---|---|---|---|
| `styles` | list of `melee`, `ranged`, `magic`, `stealth` | who may hold it; a Dagger is `["melee", "stealth"]` | any promoted hero |
| `damage` | whole number ≥ 1 | every hit before the level step, matchup and armour | 1 (a fist) |
| `speed` | `fast` / `normal` / `slow` | a swing every 2 / 3 / 4 s | `normal` |
| `hands` | `2` | a two-hander, leaving no hand for a tool | one hand |
| `type` | `weapon` | the Bank's label and tint (the editor writes it) | `tool` |

An older item's `skillRequired` naming a combat skill reads as one style; the
editor folds it into `styles` on the first change.

**A family's ability** is one library effect (`effects.json`, made in the
Rules editor) that every tier of the family references with its own `scale`
(1–5): `effects: [{ "effectId": "effect_spear", "scale": 2 }]` on an iron
spear. The scale multiplies the effect's number. Armour's own stat is the same
kind of effect (Provides Armor, flat). The rows the abilities use:

| Rules row (Provides) | Bucket | Narrowing | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pierce | flat | none | Spear: 1 |
| Accuracy | flat | none | Bow: 5 (points off the miss chance) |
| Crit Chance | flat | none | Rapier: 5 (points on the 5 % base) |
| Dodge | flat | none | a cloak: 5 (points on the enemy's miss) |
| Armor | flat | none | leather: 1 |
| Damage | flat or percentage | none | an elixir: +1 |
| Double Item Chance | flat (a %) | the item (or any item) | Cleaver: 10 on Raw Beef; one statement per meat |
| Status Stack Limit | flat (± stacks) | the status | Hide Vest: −1 on Poison |

**An enemy** (`enemy` on a Token):

| Field | Type | Meaning | Default when absent |
|---|---|---|---|
| `level` | whole number ≥ 1 | fills the numbers below from the level table | (required) |
| `style` | a combat skill | its side of the cycle | `melee` |
| `kinds` | up to two of `armoured`, `nimble`, `swift`, `brute`, `venomous` | presets over the level (below) | none |
| `hp`, `hit`, `armor`, `xp` | whole numbers | replace what the level and kinds fill | filled |
| `speed` | `fast` / `normal` / `slow` | replaces the filled swing | filled |
| `dodge`, `accuracy`, `pierce` | whole numbers | replace the filled 0 | 0 (Nimble: 15 dodge) |
| `critChance` | whole number (%) | replaces the filled 5 | 5 |
| `hostile` | `true` | ambushes heroes near it | peaceful |
| `budgetScale` | number | older content only: scales the level's HP, hit and XP | 1 |

**How a kind combines with the level** (the order the game applies): the
level table (and an older `budgetScale`), then each kind in the order ticked,
then any number typed on the enemy, which wins outright. Armoured: armour +2,
slow, same hit. Nimble: Dodge +15. Swift: fast, hit × 2/3 (rounded halves up,
at least 1). Brute: slow, hit × 4/3 (rounded) and at least 1 more than the
level's. Venomous: every landed hit adds a stack of Poison. Kinds never touch
HP or XP. Swift cannot go with Armoured or Brute (the CMS refuses it; content
that has it anyway lets the later kind's speed win).

| Level 1 (hero level 1: 11 HP) | HP | Hit | Armour | Swing | Damage per second |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| no kind | 21 | 1 | 0 | 3 s | 0.33 |
| Armoured | 21 | 1 | 2 | 4 s | 0.25 |
| Swift | 21 | 1 | 0 | 2 s | 0.5 |
| Brute | 21 | 2 | 0 | 4 s | 0.5 |
| Level 40, Swift / none / Brute | 60 | 2 / 3 / 4 | 1 | 2 / 3 / 4 s | 1 / 1 / 1 |

Every kind makes an enemy harder than its level (a kind is a twist, not a
trade): at level 1 the rounding gives Swift and Brute half again the
damage per second, and Armoured and Nimble lengthen the fight. The author
balances with the level or a typed number.

**Choices made in C2 the rulings did not spell out** (each small to change):

- **A Recruit is refused every weapon** (the equip says why), rather than
  holding one that does nothing; tools are unchanged.
- **A weapon naming no style suits any promoted hero.**
- **A weapon of another style still in a hero's hands** (from before a
  re-training, or an older save) is not swung, and its family's ability is
  off; it is not taken off automatically.
- **Venomous used the old status engine's Poison** until C4 built the real
  one (see "Statuses and boss moves" below).

## Encounters (built in C5, 2026-10-24; simplified after the owner's look; for the C0 and brief 240 authors)

*What the CMS writes and the game reads for one-on-one fights, bosses, guardians, summons and
ambushes. Nothing in `data/` uses them yet. The owner's ruling after the first look (2026-10-24):
"Too complicated, too different from normal spawns. I just want these types of enemies to have
their own spawners." So bosses and guardians come from ordinary camps, nothing is blocked, and a
summon is a Token's work.*

**An enemy** gains one field:

| Field | Type | Meaning | Default when absent |
|---|---|---|---|
| `boss` | `true` | fights only heroes sent at it: never hostile (the CMS greys Hostile), never made by a `Spawns` rule, so never an ambusher; boss moves (C4) hang on it | not a boss |

**A boss's or a guardian's camp of one** is an ordinary spawner, nothing new: `spawner: { spawns:
[{ typeId: <the enemy>, weight: 1 }], allowance: 1, intervalMs: <how often>, upkeep: [] }` (CMS:
Spawning and Building → Spawner). A Base Map brings it by listing the camp Token under its
**Enemy camps**. ⚠️ Camps keep today's timing: the clock fills while the camp is full and waits, so
a member beaten after the camp has been full a whole interval is replaced on the next tick (an open
question for the owner below).

**A guardian** is a tough enemy (tick Hostile if it should attack) whose camp of one stands by a
rich node. It blocks nothing.

**A Summoning Circle** is an ordinary station: a Token that **Works as** a Starting skill (CMS:
its Station rule) plus a **recipe** in that skill's pool (CMS: Recipes) whose input is the summon
item and whose output is the boss Token (`outputs: [{ tokenId: <the boss> }]`, the shape a
Foundation's build already uses). Every hero holds every Starting skill, so any hero may work it;
later it becomes the Summoning skill's station. The player picks its recipe once, as on any
station. Without the item it waits; it calls one boss per cycle while items last. The summon item
is any item. ⚠️ The recipe sits in that skill's whole pool, so every station of that skill offers
it too (Alchemy ships no recipes today). A Token's own Work Cycle cannot output a Token in the CMS
(only a recipe can), which is why the circle is a station.

**An ambush** is a `Spawns` rule on the node, on its own finished cycle, with a chance of about
**2%** ("When this Token finishes a cycle, a 2% chance to spawn Fire Elemental on the nearest free
spot"). The enemy it spawns attacks the worker at once (hostile or not), is beaten in one kill, and
the node springs no other while it stands. The Spawns Token list never offers a boss.

**Choices made in C5 the rulings did not spell out** (each small to change):

- **Waiting vs another enemy**: an area flag takes the next thing in its reach (its priorities
  decide, nearest first); with nothing else it waits at its flag. A pinned flag waits beside the
  enemy. Either takes it within a second of it coming free. No random roll is involved.
- **An ambusher is one fight** (1 use, whatever its type says), and **a node holds one at a time**:
  that bounds enemies alive by the number of worked ambush nodes.
- **The Summoning Circle is a station** working as a Starting skill (the QA fixture: Alchemy, level
  1), so any hero may work it with no new engine rule.
- **A beaten enemy** is said "Beaten", not "Depleted", by the hero who beat it (all enemies).

## Statuses and boss moves (built in C4, 2026-10-24; for the content author)

*What the CMS writes and the game reads. Nothing in `data/` uses them yet.*

**A status is a library effect with a Status section** (Effect editor):
"stacks up to" (1 = does not stack, landing again renews it) and "lasts
(ticks)" (0 = the whole fight). Its rules count once per stack; every status
ends with its fight and lands only on a fighter. A tick is 3 s. The two to
author, with the owner's numbers:

| Effect (name → id) | Status | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| Poison → `effect_poison` (**keep this id**: the Venomous kind names it) | stacks up to 3, lasts 0 (the fight) | On Tick: deals 1 damage to itself, ignoring armour. |
| Burn → `effect_burn` | stacks up to 1, lasts 3 ticks | On Tick: deals 1 damage to itself, ignoring armour. Provides −50 % Food Healing. |

**Fight moments** (the moment picker, offered with Applies, Deals, Heals and
Removes only; no charge, no cooldown):

| Moment | Its number | Fires | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| On Swing | every Nth (1 = every swing) | as the carrier swings, before the roll | On Every 4th Swing: applies Heavy Strike to the enemy for the swing. |
| On Hit | every Nth (1 = every hit) | after a landed hit that leaves the other side standing | On Every 3rd Hit: applies Burn to the hero. |
| On Health Below | a share, 1–99 % (default 50) | once a fight, the first time the carrier's health is below it | On Health Below 50%: heals 10 health on itself. |

On an enemy's own rules "itself" and "the enemy" are the monster and "the
hero" the hero it fights; on a hero's item "itself" and "the hero" are the
hero and "the enemy" the monster. **Applies** gains "for the swing" (until the
swing being made is over) and "the hero" as a target. **New rows**: Swing Time
(a percentage: −50 % swings twice as often) and Food Healing (a percentage of
what a meal heals in a fight), both on lingering effects.

**The four boss moves** as library effects (Heavy Strike: Provides +100 %
Damage; Stone Skin: Provides 2 Armor; Enrage: Provides −50 % Swing Time),
carried by the boss's own rules:

- On Every 4th Swing: applies Heavy Strike to the enemy for the swing.
- On Every 3rd Hit: applies Burn to the hero.
- On Health Below 50%: heals 10 health on itself. / On Health Below 50%:
  applies Stone Skin to the enemy for the fight.
- On Health Below 30%: applies Enrage to the enemy for the fight.

**Choices made in C4 the rulings did not spell out** (each small to change):

- **Burn lasts 3 ticks** (9 s): 3 damage and halved meals; landing again
  renews it, so a burn on every hit keeps it lit.
- **Statuses are library effects the CMS authors**, not a fixed code list:
  later statuses (Bleed…) need no code. Venomous names Poison by its id.
- **A status lands only on someone in a fight**; on a gatherer it does
  nothing (it would never end).
- **Venomous poisons on a hit that leaves the hero standing**, one stack a
  hit, through the same On Hit moment a boss uses.
- **"Every Nth swing" counts swings**, misses included, per fight (a fresh
  enemy starts at 1); a heavy strike that misses is spent. **On Hit** counts
  landed hits and was added beside it, since a status attack and Venomous land
  on a hit.
- **On Health Below fires once a fight**, the first time the health is below
  the share, and every rule on it sees the health that set it off (a heal
  listed first cannot cancel the armour after it).
- **One Rules tick (3 s) for everything that lingers**, not only statuses;
  nothing authored ticked on the old 5 s clock.
- **Food Healing applies to meals in a fight** (Burn exists only there).
- **Enemies read Damage (flat and %) and Swing Time from effects on them**,
  so a boss's own buffs work; gear's flat-only contributions do not carry
  Swing Time.
