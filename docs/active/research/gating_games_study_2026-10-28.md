# How other games gate new tiers (research, 2026-10-28)

*For the gating workshop ([concept_progression_ledger.md](../concept_progression_ledger.md),
"Gating: open"). Written by a research agent from its own knowledge (no web
search); read-only, nothing decided. The owner's three goals: **the player
sees it coming**, **it feels like discovery**, **it uses many skills**; plus
"fits an idle game where the player sets up and checks in".*

## Ten games, briefly

| Game | Gate | Seeing it coming | Discovery | What feels bad |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Factorio** | Research paid in science packs; each new pack colour needs a new production chain | Whole tree visible, greyed | Building the chain that makes the new pack: earned, not a surprise | Later packs feel like busywork to some |
| **Melvor Idle** | Skill levels; dungeons and boss drops, some key items | Every unlock labelled "requires level X / item Y" | Low: checklist plus rare-drop moments | Rare-drop gates; many ladders that feel interchangeable |
| **RuneScape** | Quests with skill and quest requirements; capstones chain many | Quest list shows needs; famous areas known early | High: the story reveals new lands, the reward is a place | One low skill blocks everything; wiki dependence |
| **Stardew Valley** | Community Center bundles (mixed collections); mine depth for ore tiers | Bundle board shows empty slots and reward; the broken bridge is there from day one | Very high: the world repairs itself; an empty slot for an item never yet seen | A missed seasonal item means waiting a year |
| **Terraria** | Crafted boss summons; key bosses change the world (new ores appear in old caves) | NPC hints, ominous messages, a temple door that won't open | Very high: world-changing message, old places with new ore | A hard boss wall; obscure summon recipes |
| **Minecraft** | Portals: obsidian (diamond pick); Eyes of Ender (two dimensions' drops) | A recognisable portal frame; the eye flies toward the goal | High: building the doorway; following the eye | Grinding drops; little guidance |
| **RimWorld** | Research benches needing traded or scavenged parts; endgame ship event | Visible tree; endgame goal announced | Medium–high: events drop rare parts | Waiting on the right trader |
| **Satisfactory** | Milestones bought with item batches; Space Elevator deliveries end phases | The Elevator dominates the base and shows the next delivery | Medium: the launch; hard drives with alternate recipes | Big deliveries feel like a wait |
| **Kittens Game / Antimatter Dimensions** | Things reveal themselves when near; AD hides layers until a threshold | Weak in Kittens; strong in AD (a progress bar) | Very high: the interface itself grows | Opaque; long plateaus |
| **Against the Storm** | Meta upgrade tree between runs; Glades on the map hold events | Tree visible; unopened Glades on the map | High: opening a Glade is a gamble | Random blueprints deny planned chains |

## Scores against the owner's goals (3 strong, 2 partly, 1 weak)

| Game | Seen coming | Discovery | Many skills | Set-up-and-check-in |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Factorio | 3 | 2 | 3 | 3 |
| Melvor Idle | 3 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
| RuneScape quests | 3 | 3 | 3 | 1 |
| Stardew bundles | 3 | 3 | 3 | 2 |
| Terraria bosses | 2 | 3 | 3 | 1 |
| Minecraft portals | 2 | 3 | 3 | 1 |
| RimWorld | 2 | 2 | 3 | 2 |
| Satisfactory | 3 | 2 | 3 | 3 |
| Kittens / AD | 1 / 3 | 3 | 2 | 3 |
| Against the Storm | 2 | 3 | 2 | 2 |

**Takeaway**: the games strong on all goals pair **a visible empty slot or
locked object** (bundle board, portal frame, Space Elevator, greyed tree) with
**a world change when it is filled** (bridge repaired, new ore in old caves, a
new tab). Drop-only gates (Melvor's rare keys) are closest to today's map
gate and score lowest on discovery and balance.

## Eight gate patterns, as they might look here (not ranked)

1. **Bundle or collection board**: a board of mixed items (gathered, crafted,
   looted) with a named reward. *Here*: the next plane's map as a torn
   picture with empty slots (Copper Bar ×20, Oak Plank ×20, a Wolf Pelt, a
   cooked dish); filling them writes the first iron-and-fir Region.
2. **Keystone item from several chains**: one expensive object with parts
   from different skills. *Here*: a "Map of the Iron Hills" crafted from ink
   (Alchemy), vellum (a beast hide), a compass (Smithing) and a guardian's
   fragment.
3. **Visible-but-locked thing on the mat**: the goal in plain sight from the
   start. *Here*: a sealed gate or dormant portal stone on every plane's mat,
   showing its needs on hover, lighting up stage by stage, opening the next
   plane.
4. **Boss key / guardian**: a crafted summon starts a fight that opens the
   tier. *Here*: craft an offering to call the plane's guardian; beating it
   opens the next plane, so combat is the last step, not the slow random one.
5. **World reacts / foreshadowing**: milestones change existing places; hints
   arrive before the player can act. *Here*: after the copper guardian falls,
   old Regions sprout a few iron veins; or heroes bring back "a strange grey
   stone" that can't be used yet.
6. **Research tree / greyed recipe book**: future things listed, locked, with
   needs shown. *Here*: an Atlas page shows the next plane's materials as
   silhouettes with requirements; the player plans backwards.
7. **Revealing interface / first-find unlock**: new things appear the first
   time the player gets near. *Here*: the first plane-2 material a hero
   brings back (a rare drop, a cache) opens a new Atlas page with a story
   beat; paired with a visible pattern so the player can still plan.
8. **One-time discovery sites**: findable places hold pieces of the gate.
   *Here*: Region ruins and caches hold map fragments; three plus a crafted
   binding make the next map; a sure fragment in each boss chest stops bad
   luck stalling the player.

## Notes on balance

- Hand-made lists (bundles, keystones, quest needs) balance more easily than
  drop rates: each slot is a known number of hours, not an expected value with
  long bad-luck tails.
- A **visible requirement** and a **hidden payoff** don't conflict: show what
  is needed, surprise with what it opens.
- The worst gates share one rare random drop or one low skill blocking
  everything; soften with guarantees (a sure fragment) or alternatives (any 2
  of 3 slots).
- Patterns combine: a keystone (2) built at a visible locked Token (3),
  finished by a guardian (4), followed by the world reacting (5), close to
  what RuneScape, Terraria and Stardew each do.
