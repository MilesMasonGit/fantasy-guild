# World map theorycraft (research, 2026-10-28)

*A design analyst's stress-test of [concept_world_map.md](../concept_world_map.md)
(draft) and alternatives, read-only. Nothing here is decided.*

## Stress-test of the draft

**Scale**: a hex grid has 6 cells in ring 1, 12 in ring 2, 18 in ring 3 and
24 in ring 4. A first run of 20+ Regions over 1–3 months means about 8 rings,
about 3 settled per ring: roughly 25 of ~200 cells (~12 %). The rest is
backdrop, fine if geography is coherent and noise if each cell is rolled
alone.

**Over a run**: hours 0–4 work well: ring 1 opens at the tutorial's end, 3
of 6 cells settled in ~1.5 h, ring 2's iron and fir visible with
"Mining 15 / Forestry 15" (the cartographer's three maps, with geography).
Days 1–3: ring-2 cells cost ~4–8 guild-hours each; three heroes level three
skills side by side ("the whole guild earns it"). Week 2: a cell costs 1–2
days and most of a growing frontier is out of reach (window-shopping).
Months 1–3: fog-lifting fades into routine; template prices read as chores.

**Failure modes and fixes**

1. **Iron on the wrong side**: guarantee key materials per frontier, not per
   ring (every ring-1 cell's outer neighbours include iron or fir).
2. **The draft contradicts itself** ("next ring visible from the start" vs
   "settling reveals beyond"): show the next ring's outputs early, exact
   requirements only once adjacent.
3. **Iron to reach iron**: a ring-N place costs only ring N−1 (or older)
   materials plus levels (a ledger rule).
4. **Hop tedium** (only the active Region runs): a price payable from at most
   two settled places, or one generalised place.
5. **The back of the map becomes a museum**: specialised Regions earn a
   return only if clearly denser; accept or price it in.
6. **Occupied regions**: fun at ring 1 (six goblins); at ring 4 the
   performance limits (~6 enemies alive, 1–2 fights at once) make "clear
   everything first" slow, one fight at a time, with nothing producing; and
   offline, a defeated hero waits at the Hall, so an 8-hour absence stalls
   after 10 minutes. Fix: occupy a corner or a guardian, occasionally.
7. **Stuck in front of a wall**: always one cheap same-ring sidestep on the
   frontier; deposits toward a price over time with a bar (Stardew bundles).
8. **An obvious best pick**: outputs and prices make "take the iron cell"
   obvious. Real choices come from direction (what each cell reveals),
   specialist vs generalist, layout modifiers, guardian vs materials. Offer
   2–4 real options instead of a growing grid.
9. **Still mechanical**: a hex showing "Iron, Fir" is an Iron Ore map with
   nicer framing. Cohesion needs connected geography (a mountain chain, a
   coast, a forest belt), named landmarks, and the guild's mark and roads on
   the map.
10. **The goal hides behind a screen**: the player watches the mat; pin the
    next place onto the mat.

**Fit**: hero orders' rebuilding (~8 drags plus camps) is light enough for
20+ moves if camp Foundations are cheap against income. A new world per run
suits the ritual. Offline and performance are fine except offline clearing.
Throws away map items, the Cartography slots' map chemistry and upcycling.

## Alternatives

- **A. Passes on the mat.** Each Region places 1–3 fixed edge Tokens (a
  broken bridge, an overgrown pass, a goblin-held gate, a ford), each naming
  the place beyond and its main outputs, with a painted strip of hills at the
  mat's edge. A pass is a multi-stage station: materials deposited over time
  (a bar), a Construction hero builds it, sometimes a guardian waits; the last
  stage writes the neighbouring Region. *First iron*: in the Mountain, the
  "Collapsed Mine Road" north leads to the Ashen Hills (iron, coal): 40
  planks, 20 copper bars, 15 rations, Mining 12, then the Goblin Chief. Sees
  it coming strong (on the mat from minute one), discovery good, many skills
  strong (stages are hero jobs), guild effort strong. Reuses generation,
  modifiers, treasures, terrain, travel, the tome. Moderate cost.
- **B. One persistent mat that grows outward.** The best feeling of expansion,
  but collides with the 128-Token cap, the fixed mat, catch-up time and
  moving day; needs a camera and chunking. Highest cost; not advised.
- **C. Landmarks on the horizon.** ~8 named landmarks per run, one per tier,
  visible from the start (the Smoking Mountain, the Great Fir, the Sunken
  Tower), each a 3–4 stage story project that opens its tier (road, lamps,
  the guardian at the gate → the Volcano and iron country around it). Sees it
  coming strongest, story strong, many skills strong, discovery medium.
  Cleanest ledger fit. Low–moderate cost plus authoring.
- **D. Expeditions and roads.** Heroes away on an offline timer return with
  2–3 charted places to choose from; roads trickle goods between places.
  Discovery strong, sees it coming weak; the trickle undermines "only the
  active Region runs"; the owner already declined expedition timers once.
  Best as a scouting add-on.
- *Variant*: a Slay-the-Spire route map: cheap and readable, but branches
  you can't take back hurt in a months-long run, and ones you can become a
  tech tree.

## The analyst's recommendation

A hybrid, **"places on the map, passes on the mat"**: keep the world map
screen (fog, named places, rings, guaranteed key materials, outputs shown);
put the gate on the playmat as pass Tokens whose stages the guild fills over
time (each stage a hero job, sometimes ending in a guardian); one landmark per
tier as the long-range goal on the horizon; occupation shrinks to a corner or
a guardian.
